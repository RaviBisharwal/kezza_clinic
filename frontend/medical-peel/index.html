The Treatment

What is a medical peel?

Many people try over-the-counter creams or salon facials for pigmentation and see little change. Stubborn problems — melasma, long-standing sun damage, dark marks after acne — often need a plan that treats the cause, not just the surface. A medical peel is one part of that plan.

The difference from a routine peel is the medical process around it. Your doctor first works out what kind of pigment or texture problem you have and how deep it is. The skin is then primed for a few weeks with prescribed creams and sunscreen, which makes results more even and lowers the risk of dark marks. The peel itself is selected for the condition — doctors may use combination depigmenting peels, or low-strength trichloroacetic acid (TCA) or Jessner's peels — and applied in measured layers.

Medical peels are usually spaced about a month apart, which is why the price at Kezza is ₹5,000 per month. How many sessions you need is decided by your consultant, based on how your skin responds.

For conditions like melasma there is no one-time cure. Peels can lighten it, and continued sun protection and maintenance keep it under control. Laser toning with a Q-switch laser is sometimes combined with peels in the same plan.

Suitability

Who is a medical peel for?

A medical peel may suit you if

  • You have pigmentation or marks that have not improved with creams
  • You have been diagnosed with melasma and want it lighter and controlled
  • You can follow a priming routine and strict sun protection
  • You can plan for a few days of peeling after each session

Tell your doctor first if

  • You are pregnant or breastfeeding — many depigmenting treatments are postponed
  • You take isotretinoin or have stopped it recently
  • You have sensitive skin, rosacea, eczema or a history of dark marks after treatments
  • You get cold sores, or have waxed, threaded or had laser recently
  • You have used steroid creams on your face

Bleaching creams and unprescribed steroid creams can make pigmentation worse over time. Bring any creams you have used to your consultation.

Step by Step

Your medical peel plan

  1. 01Diagnosis

    Skin examination

    Your doctor examines your skin, asks about triggers, creams and medicines, and confirms the diagnosis.

  2. 02Priming

    2–4 weeks

    Prescribed creams and daily sunscreen prepare your skin for a more even response.

  3. 03Peel session

    Doctor-applied

    The chosen peel is applied in measured layers and timed; warmth or stinging is normal.

  4. 04Post-peel care

    Days 1–7

    Peeling and dryness are common. Use the barrier cream and sunscreen advised, and do not pick.

  5. 05Monthly review

    Next session

    Your doctor reviews progress at each monthly visit and adjusts the plan.

  6. 06Maintenance

    Keeping results

    Once you reach your goal, maintenance care and sun protection help results last.

Safety

Side effects and safety

Expected after a medical peel

  • Warmth, stinging and redness during and after the peel
  • Peeling, dryness and tightness for several days
  • Temporary darkening of pigment before it lightens

Less common

  • Dark patches (post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation), especially with sun exposure
  • Irritation, allergy or a cold sore flare
  • Blistering or crusting; rarely scarring
  • Melasma coming back if sun protection and maintenance stop

Contact the clinic if your skin blisters, becomes very painful or swollen, or shows signs of infection after a peel.
