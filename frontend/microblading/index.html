Microblading in Jaipur | Eyebrow PMU & Hybrid Brows

Microblading is a semi-permanent eyebrow treatment in which fine, hair-like strokes of pigment are placed in the upper layer of the skin to make brows look fuller and more even. At Kezza, eyebrow permanent makeup (PMU) is done by our trained PMU artist at our Jaipur and Sikar clinics, after a patch test and a brow design that you approve.

The Technique

What Is Microblading?

In microblading, the artist uses a small handheld tool with a row of very fine needles rather than a tattoo machine. Each stroke makes a tiny, shallow nick in the top layer of the dermis and leaves pigment behind, drawn in the direction your natural brow hairs grow, so the brows look filled in without looking painted on.

Because the pigment sits closer to the surface than a traditional tattoo, microblading looks softer and fades over time. A review in the journal Skin Appendage Disorders describes microblading as temporary, lasting up to three years depending on the pigment used, while traditional tattoos can last far longer.

Eyebrow PMU also includes powder (shaded) brows, in which tiny dots of pigment build a soft, filled-in look, and hybrid brows, which combine hair strokes at the front with shading through the body and tail of the brow. Hybrid brows suit people who want natural texture with a little more definition.

In Short:

  • Hair-like strokes: drawn by hand to match your brows.
  • Semi-permanent: fades gradually and needs a refresh.
  • Design first: you approve the shape before any pigment.

Suitability

Is Microblading Right for You?

Eyebrow PMU suits most healthy adults with sparse or uneven brows. Your skin type decides which technique will heal best.

Eyebrow PMU may suit you if

  • Your brows are thin, sparse, uneven or over-plucked
  • You fill in your brows every day and would like to save that time
  • You have small gaps from old scars or patchy growth that you want to disguise
  • You want a soft, natural look that fades gradually rather than a lasting tattoo

Wait, or check with us first, if

  • You are pregnant or breastfeeding; we recommend waiting until afterwards
  • You have taken isotretinoin for acne recently, or have eczema, psoriasis, vitiligo, a rash or an infection on or near the brows
  • You form keloids or raised scars easily, have a bleeding disorder or take blood-thinning medicines
  • You have very oily skin or large pores on the brows; powder or hybrid brows usually heal better than hair strokes

If your brow hair has been lost to scarring or over-plucking and you would like real hair to grow there, ask about an eyebrow transplant instead.

Procedure

How a Microblading Appointment Works

Much of your first appointment is spent on consultation, brow design and numbing, before any pigment is used.

01

Consultation & Patch Test

At a consultation before your treatment day, the artist talks through the look you want, checks your skin and medical history, and does a patch test so any reaction has time to show.

02

Brow Mapping

Your brows are measured and drawn to suit your face. Pigment is applied only after you approve the shape and the colour.

03

Numbing & Preparation

A numbing cream is applied, the skin is cleaned with antiseptic and a new disposable blade is opened for you.

04

Strokes or Shading

Hair strokes, shading or both are built up in layers, following the direction of your natural brow hairs.

05

Touch-Up at 4 to 6 Weeks

Once the skin has healed, a touch-up session fills any gaps and fine-tunes the colour, shape and density.

Microblading, powder or hybrid brows: how to choose

The best technique depends mostly on your skin and the look you want. Hair strokes show up crisply on normal to dry skin, and they are ideal for filling gaps in brows that still have some hair. On oily skin, strokes can blur and fade faster as the skin heals; a medical review of eyebrow microblading notes that people with very active oil glands tend to hold the pigment less well. Powder and hybrid brows are usually a better choice there. If you like a soft, made-up brow look, powder brows give that finish, while hybrid brows suit people who want natural texture at the front and more definition through the arch.

Before your appointment

  • Avoid sun tanning on the face and any peels or laser treatments near the brows in the weeks before.
  • Tell us about medicines you take, including blood thinners and acne tablets. Do not stop a prescribed medicine without asking your doctor.
  • Let your brows grow naturally for a while so the artist can see your natural shape and hair pattern.
  • Come without brow makeup, and bring photos of brows you like to help with the design.

Aftercare in the first weeks

Healing brows need a little patience. For about a week, keep the area clean and dry apart from the cleaning routine you are given, use only the ointment recommended by the clinic, and avoid makeup on the brows, swimming, saunas, steam and workouts that make you sweat heavily. Protect the brows from direct sun. Flaking and itching are normal from around the third day; picking the flakes can pull pigment out and leave gaps. Once healed, a daily sunscreen on the brows and avoiding exfoliating acids and retinoids on the area help the colour last longer. Tell any doctor or therapist about your eyebrow PMU before laser or peel treatments on the face.

Risks and how they are reduced

Most people heal without problems, but eyebrow PMU is a cosmetic tattoo and carries real, if uncommon, risks. Infection is reduced with sterile technique and a new disposable blade for every client. A patch test before treatment can pick up an early reaction to the pigment, although no test can rule out every allergic reaction. Some people develop raised scars or keloids, so tell us if you scar easily. Rarely, pigment can trigger small lumps (granulomas) months or years later; any lump or persistent redness in the brows should be seen by a doctor. Pigment can also change colour as it fades, turning cooler or warmer over time. Faded, discoloured or misplaced brow PMU can often be improved with a PMU correction plan, discussed at a permanent makeup consultation.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does microblading cost in Jaipur?
At Kezza Clinic, microblading costs ₹12,000, which covers 2 sessions over about a month, with our PMU artist Krishna Choudhary at our Jaipur and Sikar clinics.
How long does microblading last?
Microblading usually lasts 12 to 18 months before it needs a refresh, and eyebrow PMU in general can last one to three years. It fades faster on oily skin, with a lot of sun exposure, and with exfoliating products, peels or laser treatments on the brow area.
Does microblading hurt?
A numbing cream is applied before the treatment, so most people describe the feeling as pressure or light scratching rather than pain. The brows can feel tender, like a mild graze, for a day or two afterwards.
What is the difference between microblading and powder brows?
Microblading creates individual hair strokes by hand, which look very natural on normal to dry skin. Powder brows use tiny dots of pigment to create a soft, shaded look that usually heals more evenly and lasts longer on oily skin. Hybrid brows combine the two. The artist suggests a technique after looking at your skin and brows.
Is microblading safe?
Problems are uncommon when sterile, single-use tools and a patch test are used, but no cosmetic tattoo is free of risk. Possible complications include infection, allergic reactions to the pigment, raised scars or keloids, and small lumps called granulomas. The US Food and Drug Administration notes that no colour additives are approved for injection into the skin, so pigment choice and hygiene matter. A patch test can show an early reaction, although it cannot rule out every later one.
How do I look after my brows while they heal?
For the first week, keep the brows clean and follow the cleaning routine you are given, apply only the ointment the clinic recommends, and avoid makeup, heavy sweating, swimming, steam and direct sun on the area. Let flakes fall off on their own. Printed aftercare instructions are given after every treatment.
Can I have an MRI scan after microblading?
Yes. The FDA notes that swelling or burning in tattooed or permanent makeup areas during an MRI is uncommon, and that the risks of avoiding an MRI your doctor has recommended are likely to be much greater. Tell the radiographer about your eyebrow PMU before the scan.
