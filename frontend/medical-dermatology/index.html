Expert Answers

Frequently Asked Medical Questions

Clear answers to common questions about medical dermatology, rashes, growths, and clinical treatments

When should I see a dermatologist instead of using an over-the-counter cream?
You should consult a qualified dermatologist whenever a skin condition persists beyond two weeks, spreads rapidly, causes pain or itching, or fails to respond to simple moisturizers. Self-medicating with over-the-counter creams that contain strong steroids often leads to steroid-induced rosacea, skin thinning, and stubborn fungal resistance.
Are mole, skin tag, and wart removals safe and scar-free at Kezza?
Every skin growth is first examined with a polarized dermatoscope to confirm it is completely benign. Harmless tags and warts are removed using high-precision radiofrequency electrosurgery under local anaesthetic. The procedure takes only minutes, causes minimal discomfort, seals blood vessels immediately, and heals with very subtle marks.
Can chronic conditions like psoriasis and eczema be managed effectively?
Psoriasis and atopic eczema are chronic inflammatory immune-mediated disorders. While they cannot be permanently eradicated with a single quick fix, our doctor-led treatment protocols provide long-term remission, clear thickened scales, restore stratum corneum barrier lipids, and dramatically reduce flare frequency.
Are clinical peels and laser treatments safe for sensitive Indian skin (Fitzpatrick IV-VI)?
Yes. Indian skin requires customized, conservative treatment parameters to avoid post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH). At Kezza, our dermatologists use gentle pH-balanced peeling agents and targeted Q-switched lasers specifically calibrated for melanin-rich skin tones.
What is included in a clinical dermatology consultation at Kezza?
Your consultation includes an in-depth clinical evaluation by a senior MD/DVD dermatologist, polarized dermatoscope examination of affected lesions, diagnosis of underlying triggers, a comprehensive written medical prescription, and follow-up guidance.
