Botox & Dermal Fillers in Jaipur | Anti-Wrinkle Injections & Volume

Botox and dermal fillers are the two main cosmetic injections for the face. Botox, the best-known brand of botulinum toxin, relaxes the muscles behind expression lines such as frown lines and crow's feet, while hyaluronic acid fillers restore volume in the lips, cheeks, under-eyes and smile lines. How They Work

Botox or Fillers: What Is the Difference?

Botox and fillers treat different things. Botox is a prescription medicine that relaxes the muscles that cause lines when you move, so it suits frown lines, forehead lines and crow's feet. Fillers are gels, usually hyaluronic acid, injected under the skin to restore volume in the lips, cheeks, under-eyes and smile lines.

Expression lines form where muscles contract again and again under the skin. Botulinum toxin blocks the signal from the nerve to the muscle, so the treated muscle relaxes and the skin over it smooths out. It works best on lines that appear when you move your face, starts working within a few days and usually lasts about three to four months. Several brands of botulinum toxin type A exist, and their doses are not interchangeable, so the dose is planned for the product used and the strength of your muscles.

Dermal fillers work differently. Hyaluronic acid is a sugar found naturally in the skin; as a gel, it adds volume where it is placed and attracts water. The change is visible straight away, and the body gradually breaks the gel down, so results usually last four to twelve months, depending on the area and the product. Hyaluronic acid fillers can also be dissolved with an enzyme if needed.

Many faces benefit from one or the other, and some from both: Botox for lines in the upper face and filler for volume loss in the mid and lower face. Neither lifts loose skin or changes skin texture or pigmentation, which need other treatments.

In Short:

  • Botox: relaxes lines caused by movement.
  • Fillers: restore volume and shape.
  • Doctor-given: planned after a face assessment.

Areas Commonly Treated

Crow's Feet

Lines at the outer corners of the eyes that show when you smile or squint, softened with Botox.

Relaxing a strong jaw muscle with Botox can soften a square jaw; this is planned carefully by the doctor.

Lips

Hyaluronic acid filler adds subtle volume, shape or hydration to thin or uneven lips.

Under-Eye Hollows

Small amounts of filler can soften tear-trough hollows after the cause of dark circles is checked.

Suitability

Are Botox or Fillers Right for You?

Both suit healthy adults who want a subtle, temporary change and understand the risks.

Injections may suit you if

  • You have lines that appear when you frown, raise your brows or smile
  • Your lips, cheeks or under-eyes have lost volume, or you would like subtle definition
  • You prefer a natural result that keeps your expressions
  • You understand the effect is temporary and needs repeat treatment

They are not suitable, or need care, if

  • You are pregnant or breastfeeding
  • You have a nerve or muscle condition such as myasthenia gravis, or already have drooping eyelids or weak facial muscles
  • You have an infection, a cold sore or inflamed skin where the injections would go
  • You have a bleeding disorder or take blood thinners, or have had Botox in the last few months; tell your doctor first
  • You have had an allergic reaction to a filler, lidocaine or botulinum toxin before

Dark circles have several causes, and filler helps only some of them. See our dark circles treatment page before choosing under-eye filler.

Procedure

What Happens at Your Appointment

The injections take only minutes; most of the visit is the assessment and planning.

01

Face Assessment

Your doctor looks at your face at rest and in movement, checks your medical history, medicines and any previous injections, and discusses the look you want.

02

Plan & Consent

You agree on the areas, the product, the expected result, the possible side effects and the cost before anything is injected.

03

Preparation

The skin is cleaned and the points are marked. Numbing cream can be used, and many fillers contain a local anaesthetic.

04

Injections

Botox is injected in tiny amounts with a very fine needle; filler is placed in small amounts with a fine needle or a blunt cannula.

05

Aftercare & Review

You get aftercare advice, and a review at about two weeks checks the result once Botox has taken full effect and filler swelling has settled.

What Botox and fillers can and cannot do

Botulinum toxin is very good at softening lines caused by movement, especially frown lines, forehead lines and crow's feet. Hyaluronic acid filler is very good at restoring volume: fuller lips, softer smile lines, lifted-looking cheeks and less hollow under-eyes. Neither treatment lifts sagging skin on the jaw and neck, removes deep fixed wrinkles completely or changes skin texture and pigmentation. At your consultation, your doctor explains which of your concerns injections can help with and which need a different approach, such as pigmentation treatment or skin resurfacing.

Dermal fillers by area

  • Lips: small amounts add shape, symmetry or hydration. Results usually last about six months to a year, because the lips move a lot.
  • Cheeks and midface: filler placed over the cheekbones restores volume lost with age or weight change, which can also soften the folds below. Results often last about a year.
  • Smile lines: the folds from the nose to the corners of the mouth can be softened, often by restoring cheek volume first rather than filling the fold alone.
  • Under-eyes: the skin here is thin and the area is delicate. Filler helps hollows, not pigment or puffiness, and it can persist longer than expected, so it is used only after the cause of dark circles is clear.

Safety and choosing where to be treated

Botulinum toxin is a prescription medicine, and the dose, the product and the exact injection points all matter. Mayo Clinic lists pain, swelling or bruising at the injection site, headache and flu-like symptoms as common side effects, and droopy eyelids or uneven eyebrows as possible effects when the medicine reaches nearby muscles. For fillers, bruising, swelling, redness, tenderness and small lumps are common and usually settle within one to two weeks. Infection, persistent lumps and allergic reactions are less common.

The most serious filler risk is rare: injection into a blood vessel, which can damage the skin and, very rarely, cause vision loss or stroke. The US Food and Drug Administration advises seeking immediate medical attention for unusual pain, vision changes or skin near the injection turning white, grey or blue. These risks are lowered by treatment from a trained doctor who knows facial anatomy, products from a reliable source, and a clinic that can act quickly, including dissolving hyaluronic acid filler. Never buy filler online or have injections at a salon or a home party.

Tips for your first treatment

  • Book at least two weeks before an important event, so the full result and any review are done in time.
  • Avoid alcohol and, if your doctor agrees, medicines that increase bruising for a day or two beforehand. Do not stop prescribed medicines without asking.
  • Tell the doctor if you get cold sores, as filler around the lips can trigger them.
  • Bring photos if you have a particular look in mind, or of how you looked a few years ago.

Aftercare

You can return to most activities straight away. After Botox, avoid rubbing or pressing on the treated area for 12 hours, stay upright for three to four hours and avoid strenuous exercise for 24 hours. After filler, wait until the next day to exercise, avoid sun and heat, and do not press on or massage the area unless your doctor asks you to. A cold pack wrapped in cloth helps swelling. Contact the clinic if you notice a drooping eyelid, unevenness or swelling that lasts more than a week, fever, or any increasing pain.

What affects the cost of Botox and fillers?

For Botox, the cost depends on the number of areas treated, the dose each area needs and the product used. For fillers, it depends on the areas, the amount of filler needed and the product. Treating one area costs less than treating several, and your doctor explains the plan and the total before any injection.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does Botox last?
For most people the effect lasts about three to four months, according to Mayo Clinic, and some find it lasts a little longer. Movement returns gradually, and the lines slowly return to how they looked before treatment.
When will I see the results of Botox?
Mayo Clinic notes that the injections usually begin working one to three days after treatment, and the full result can take a week or more. Cleveland Clinic says full results are visible within 10 to 14 days, which is why reviews are booked at about two weeks.
Will I look frozen?
Not when the dose is planned for your face. The aim is to soften lines while keeping natural expressions. If you prefer a lighter effect, your doctor can start with a lower dose and review it at two weeks.
What are the side effects of Botox?
Common side effects are mild pain, swelling or bruising at the injection sites, and sometimes a headache or flu-like feeling. Less often, the medicine reaches nearby muscles and causes a drooping eyelid or uneven eyebrows, which is temporary. Rarely, the effect spreads further; seek urgent care for muscle weakness, any difficulty breathing, swallowing or speaking, or vision problems.
How long do lip fillers last?
Hyaluronic acid lip filler usually lasts about six months to a year, which is shorter than in less mobile areas such as the cheeks, where results often last about a year. How long it lasts also depends on the product and on your body.
Can dermal fillers be reversed?
Hyaluronic acid fillers can be dissolved with an enzyme called hyaluronidase, which is also used urgently if filler blocks a blood vessel. The full effect can take up to two weeks, and allergic reactions to the enzyme are rare. Non-hyaluronic acid fillers cannot be dissolved this way.
Can fillers cause blindness?
Very rarely. If filler is accidentally injected into a blood vessel, it can block blood flow and cause skin damage, vision loss or stroke. A 2024 review found the nose, forehead and the area between the brows to be the highest-risk sites. Careful technique and knowledge of facial anatomy lower the risk, and urgent care is needed for unusual pain, vision changes or white, grey or blue skin near the injection.
Do I need Botox or fillers?
It depends on what bothers you. Lines that appear when you move, such as frown lines and crow's feet, usually respond to Botox. Hollows, thin lips and folds that are visible at rest usually need filler. Your doctor will explain which, if either, suits your face.
What should I avoid after Botox or fillers?
After Botox, Cleveland Clinic advises not rubbing the area for 12 hours, staying upright for three to four hours and avoiding strenuous exercise for 24 hours. After filler, the American Academy of Dermatology suggests waiting until the next day to exercise, staying out of the sun and not touching the treated area for three days.
