The Treatment

What is a Q-switched laser?

'Q-switched' describes how the laser releases its energy: in pulses that last only a few billionths of a second (nanoseconds). Energy delivered that fast creates a photo-acoustic effect — a tiny shock wave — that breaks pigment particles apart rather than slowly heating the skin. The fragments are then cleared by the body over the following weeks.

The most common type is the Q-switched Nd:YAG laser. Its 1064 nm wavelength reaches deeper pigment and is the usual choice for Indian skin, because it is absorbed less by the skin's own surface melanin. A 532 nm wavelength is used for some superficial brown spots and red or orange ink.

Laser toning is a gentle way of using the 1064 nm laser: low-energy passes over the whole face, repeated over several sessions, to lighten diffuse pigment such as melasma. It is one part of melasma care, alongside sunscreen and creams — not a cure on its own. Read more on our pigmentation and melasma treatment page.

A Q-switched laser is not a hair-removal laser and does not resurface the skin. For unwanted hair see laser hair removal; for acne scars and texture see microneedling.

Uses

What a Q-switch laser can treat

Your doctor confirms the diagnosis first, because different pigment problems respond very differently.

Freckles & sun spots

Flat brown spots on the surface of the skin (lentigines) often respond well, sometimes in fewer sessions.

Melasma (laser toning)

Low-energy toning can lighten melasma as part of a combined plan with sunscreen and creams; relapse is common without care.

Melasma treatment

Dark marks & uneven tone

Some marks left after acne or irritation can be lightened gradually, alongside creams and sun protection.

Deeper (dermal) pigment

Conditions such as nevus of Ota or ABNOM sit deeper in the skin and need a longer series of sessions.

Tattoo & PMU ink

Ink is broken into fragments the body clears; black and dark blue respond best, and several sessions are needed.

Combined skin plans

Laser sessions can be combined with peels or prescription creams when your doctor recommends it.

Suitability

Is Q-switch laser right for you?

It may suit you if

  • You have freckles, sun spots or another pigment problem diagnosed by a doctor
  • You want to lighten melasma as part of a long-term plan, not a one-off fix
  • You have an unwanted tattoo or PMU and understand it takes several sessions
  • You can protect your skin from the sun between sessions

Tell your doctor first if

  • A spot is new, changing, bleeding or itchy — it must be examined before any laser
  • You have a fresh tan or recent sunburn
  • You have taken isotretinoin, or use medicines that make the skin sensitive to light
  • You have had keloids, cold sores near the area, or an active skin infection
  • You are pregnant

Melasma is influenced by hormones, heat and light. A laser can lighten it, but sunscreen and follow-up care are what keep it under control.

Step by Step

How a Q-switch laser session works

  1. 01Consultation

    Diagnosis and plan

    Dr. Neelam examines your skin and the pigment, asks about medicines and sun exposure, and explains how many sessions are likely.

  2. 02Test spot

    When needed

    A small test area may be treated first to check how your skin responds.

  3. 03Preparation

    Clean skin, eye shields

    Makeup is removed and you wear protective eye shields throughout the session.

  4. 04Laser

    10–20 minutes for the face

    The laser passes over the area; most people feel a light snapping or warm prickling. Numbing cream can be used for tattoos.

  5. 05Aftercare

    Calm and protect

    The skin is cooled and moisturised. Use sunscreen daily and avoid harsh products for a few days.

  6. 06Next session

    Every few weeks

    Sessions are spaced a few weeks apart — longer for tattoos — and progress is reviewed at each visit.

Safety

Side effects and safety

Common and short-lived

  • Redness, warmth or mild swelling for a few hours
  • Darkening and flaking of treated spots for about a week
  • Pinpoint bleeding or crusting after tattoo sessions

Less common

  • Darker patches (post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation), especially after sun exposure
  • Lighter spots, including mottled white spots after frequent toning
  • Blistering or crusting; rarely scarring
  • Melasma returning or worsening after treatment stops
  • Incomplete removal of tattoo ink, or lighter skin where ink was

Contact the clinic if you develop blisters, open sores, signs of infection or worsening darkness after a laser session.
