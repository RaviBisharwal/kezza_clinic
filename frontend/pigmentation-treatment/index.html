THE TRUTH ABOUT SALON BLEACHES VS MEDICAL CARE

Why Medical Treatment Outperforms Salon Bleaches

Most people with dark patches or tanning first try salon facials or over-the-counter “fairness” bleaches. While these harsh chemicals strip your skin’s top layer for a quick 2-day glow, they never reach deep pigment roots. Over time, bleaching damages your natural skin barrier — causing melasma and dark spots to return even darker when exposed to sunlight. At Kezza Clinic, our doctors use gentle medical lasers and custom peels to lighten pigment safely, with a maintenance plan to keep it under control.

CLINICAL QUESTIONS & ANSWERS

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions answered by our skin doctors.

How many sessions of Q-Switched Laser Toning are needed for melasma?
Most patients see a noticeable reduction in pigmentation within 4 to 6 sessions, spaced 2 to 3 weeks apart. Custom maintenance visits at 6-8 week intervals help maintain results since melasma is influenced by hormonal and UV factors.
Can melasma return after treatment, and how is recurrence prevented?
Melasma has internal hormonal and UV-driven triggers, meaning maintenance is essential. We combine medical tyrosinase inhibitors, prescription topicals, oral antioxidants, and strict broad-spectrum mineral sunscreen application to prevent rebound hyperpigmentation.
Is laser toning painful, and does it require downtime?
Laser toning is exceptionally gentle and feels like tiny warm prickles on the skin. There is zero peeling, no crusting, and zero social downtime—you can return to work immediately after your session.
Are these pigmentation procedures safe for darker Indian skin types (Fitzpatrick IV-VI)?
Yes. Our Q-Switched Nd:YAG 1064nm laser operates in the photoacoustic nanosecond domain rather than thermal millisecond mode. This allows the laser to shatter melanin pigment granules with less heating of the surrounding skin, which lowers the risk of post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH).
Why do salon bleaches and unfairness creams make melasma worse?
Over-the-counter fairness creams and salon bleaches often contain harsh chemicals or unmonitored steroids. While they may cause temporary superficial bleaching for a few days, they destroy the natural epidermal barrier, cause rebound darkening, skin thinning, and can even induce irreversible exogenous ochronosis.
