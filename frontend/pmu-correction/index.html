PMU Correction in Jaipur | Fixing Old Permanent Makeup

Permanent makeup correction improves brows, eyeliner or lips that have faded unevenly, changed colour or were given a shape that no longer suits you. Depending on the problem, the plan may be a colour correction, a reshaping, or lightening the old pigment first. At Kezza, corrections are planned by our PMU artist at our Jaipur and Sikar clinics.

Why Correction

Why Old Permanent Makeup Needs Correcting

Permanent makeup pigments do not fade evenly. As they break down in the skin, some colours fade faster than others, so brows that started brown can turn grey, blue-green, red or orange over the years. A medical review of eyebrow microblading notes that greenish discolouration and unexpected darkening of brows can both happen.

Shape is the other common problem. Brows may have been drawn too thick, too high, too square or uneven, eyeliner may have spread beyond the lash line, and lip colour may have healed patchy or outside the natural border. Trends also change, and a shape that suited you years ago may not suit you now.

Correction is planned around what the old pigment is doing. Light or faded work can often be refreshed or re-coloured. Warm-toned pigment can balance grey or blue tones, and cooler pigment can balance red or orange. When old work is too dark, too dense or far outside the shape you want, it usually needs lightening first, so that new pigment is not simply added on top.

In Short:

  • Colour shifts: old pigment can turn grey, red or orange.
  • Shape problems: too thick, too high or uneven.
  • Plan first: refresh, re-colour or lighten before new work.

Discoloured Pigment

Suitability

Is PMU Correction Right for You?

Most old permanent makeup can be improved, but the right first step depends on how dark the old pigment is and where it sits.

Correction may suit you if

  • Your old PMU has faded unevenly or looks patchy
  • The colour has changed to grey, blue, red or orange
  • The shape no longer suits you or the two sides do not match
  • You had PMU done elsewhere and would like it improved

Lightening or a doctor's review may come first if

  • The old pigment is very dark or dense, or sits far outside the shape you want
  • The area has lumps, persistent redness, itching or swelling, which a doctor should check
  • Your PMU is recent and not fully healed; it needs several weeks to settle before it can be judged
  • You are pregnant or breastfeeding; we recommend waiting until afterwards

If you are starting fresh rather than correcting old work, see our pages on microblading, permanent eyeliner and lip blush.

Procedure

How a PMU Correction Works

Correction starts with an honest look at the old work and a step-by-step plan.

01

Assessment

The artist examines the old pigment in good light, notes its colour, depth and shape, and takes photos.

02

Your Plan

You agree on the goal and the route: refresh, re-colour, reshape, or lighten first. Some corrections take more than one stage.

03

Patch Test

A patch test, done ahead of the correction session, checks how your skin reacts to the pigments planned.

04

Correction Session

Correcting colours and new pigment are applied, often in stages to see how each layer heals.

05

Heal & Touch-Up

The area heals for four to six weeks, and a follow-up session refines the result.

How colour correction works

Colour correction uses the colour wheel. Pigment that has turned grey or blue looks warmer and more natural when a warm, orange-based corrector is placed over it, while red or orange brows are balanced with a cooler, olive-based tone. Once the unwanted tone is neutral, a new brown shade can be added that suits your hair and skin. Because the old pigment is still in the skin, the result depends on how strong it is, which is why the artist may work in stages and check each layer after it heals.

When lightening comes first

If old PMU is too dark, too wide or in the wrong place, adding more pigment on top only makes it heavier. In these cases the old pigment is usually lightened first, with laser or a non-laser method, over one or more sessions several weeks apart. The FDA notes that laser removal needs multiple treatments with a few weeks in between, and that complete removal is not always possible. Many cosmetic pigments, including browns, reds, pinks, whites and flesh tones, can turn grey or black with laser, and darkened pigment can be harder to remove, so a test area is treated first. Non-laser lightening methods have less research behind them and can cause scarring or changes in skin colour, so they also need an experienced practitioner. Once the area is lighter and fully healed, new work can be planned.

Correcting eyeliner and lips

Eyeliner that has spread or become too thick is one of the harder corrections, because the eyelid is delicate and close to the eye. Often the aim is to make the line look crisper and more even rather than to remove it completely, and any laser treatment near the eye must be done by a doctor with the eye protected. Faded or patchy lip colour can usually be refreshed, and dark or uneven old lip work may be balanced with neutralizing colours before a new tint is added.

Aftercare

Aftercare after a correction is the same as after new permanent makeup: keep the area clean and follow the routine you are given, use only the recommended ointment, avoid makeup, sweat, swimming and sun on the area for about a week, and let flakes fall off on their own. Colour can look strong at first and uneven while healing, so wait until the area has fully healed before judging the result.

What affects the cost of a PMU correction?

The cost depends on the area, how dark and widespread the old pigment is, and how many stages the correction needs. A simple refresh costs less than a correction that needs lightening and several colour sessions. The artist explains the likely number of sessions and the total at your consultation.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you fix permanent makeup done at another clinic?
Yes. Many corrections are for work done elsewhere. The artist will look at the old pigment, explain what can realistically be achieved and suggest a plan, which may take more than one session.
Do I need removal before a correction?
Not always. Light or faded PMU can often be corrected by adding the right colour or reshaping. Work that is very dark, dense or far outside the shape you want usually needs lightening first, so the new pigment can look clean.
Can laser remove permanent makeup?
Sometimes, but cosmetic pigments need extra care. The US Food and Drug Administration notes that flesh-coloured and white pigments can turn black when treated with laser, and that darkened pigment may then not respond to laser. Brown, red and pink cosmetic pigments can also darken, so a test spot, done by a doctor experienced in tattoo removal, is treated first.
How many sessions does a correction take?
A simple refresh may need one session and a touch-up. Colour correction, reshaping or lightening often takes two or more stages, spaced several weeks apart so each can heal before the next.
Can you create a beauty spot?
Yes. A small, natural-looking beauty spot can be created on clear skin, with a shade matched to your skin. We do not tattoo over an existing mole unless a doctor has checked it, because pigment can hide changes that need medical attention.
Will the result look natural?
That is always the aim, but the result depends on the old work. Some pigment can be fully corrected, while very dark or widespread work may be improved rather than completely hidden. The artist will be honest about what to expect before you start.
