Understanding Scars

Why Does Acne Leave Scars?

Acne scar treatment improves pitted and raised scars by releasing tethered skin and stimulating new collagen. The main options are subcision, microneedling radiofrequency (MNRF), fractional laser and chemical peels, usually combined and matched to your scar types. Most people need several sessions a few weeks apart, and scars become less noticeable rather than disappearing.

Acne scars are the skin's repair marks. When a pimple, nodule or cyst is deep and inflamed, it breaks down collagen fibres in the dermis, the supportive layer under the surface. As the skin heals it may make too little new collagen, which leaves a dent (an atrophic scar), or too much, which leaves a raised scar.

Scarring is more likely with moderate to severe acne, with cysts and nodules, when spots are picked or squeezed, and when effective treatment is delayed. Smoking is also linked with more severe scarring. Treating acne early is the most reliable way to prevent new scars, which is why any active breakouts are brought under control before scar treatment starts.

Flat brown or red marks left behind after a pimple are often mistaken for scars. These marks, called post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation or erythema, usually fade with time, sun protection and the right skin care. True scars change the texture of the skin, so they need procedures that rebuild or release the tissue underneath.

In Short:

  • Dents or bumps: true scars change the skin's texture.
  • Flat dark marks: usually fade and are treated differently.
  • Clear acne first: new breakouts can leave new scars.
Scar Types

Types of Acne Scars We Treat

Ice Pick Scars

Narrow, deep pits that can look like large open pores. Their depth makes them the hardest type to lift, so they are often treated one by one.

Boxcar Scars

Wider round or oval dents with sharp edges, common on the cheeks and temples. Shallow boxcar scars respond well to resurfacing.

Rolling Scars

Broad, shallow dips with sloping edges that give the skin a wavy look, because bands of scar tissue pull the surface down from below.

Raised Scars

Firm hypertrophic scars and keloids, seen more often on the jawline, chest and back. They need a different approach from dented scars.

Mixed Scarring

Most people have more than one scar type, which is why plans usually combine two or more treatments.

Marks & Redness

Brown or red marks without a dent are treated with skin care, peels or laser toning rather than scar procedures.

Suitability

Is Acne Scar Treatment Right for You?

Scar treatment works best once acne is under control and you know what each option can realistically do.

Scar treatment may suit you if

  • Your acne has been under control for a few months, with few or no new breakouts
  • You have dented scars (ice pick, boxcar or rolling) on the face, or raised scars that bother you
  • You can protect your skin from the sun while you are having treatment
  • You are looking for steady improvement over several sessions rather than a one-visit fix

Check with your doctor first if

  • You still have active, inflamed acne; this is treated first
  • You take, or have recently taken, isotretinoin or other acne medicines; your doctor needs to know every acne treatment you have used in the last two years
  • You tend to form keloids or thick raised scars after cuts or piercings
  • You are pregnant, have cold sores or another skin infection in the area, or have a condition that slows healing

Darker skin tones are more likely to develop temporary dark patches (post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation) after some procedures. Your doctor chooses the treatment, its settings, the gap between sessions and any skin preparation with this in mind.

Procedure

How Acne Scar Treatment Works at Kezza

Your plan is built around your scar types, your skin type and how much downtime you can manage.

01

Skin Examination

Your doctor looks at your scars in good light, identifies each type, checks for active acne and takes photos to track progress.

02

Preparing the Skin

Active acne is treated first. Some people follow a prescribed skin care routine for a few weeks before resurfacing to lower the risk of dark marks.

03

Releasing Tethered Scars

Rolling scars that are pulled down by fibrous bands can be released with subcision, in which a fine needle frees the bands under local anaesthetic.

04

Resurfacing

Microneedling radiofrequency, fractional laser or chemical peels stimulate new collagen to smooth the surface. Numbing cream is applied before microneedling or laser.

05

Review & Repeat

Sessions are spaced several weeks apart, and your doctor compares photos at each visit to fine-tune the plan.

Treatments for each type of scar

There is no single best treatment for acne scars. Mayo Clinic notes that no single treatment is best for everyone, and that one treatment or a combination may be used depending on the type of scarring. These are the main options your doctor may use:

  • Subcision for rolling scars. A fine needle is passed under the scar to release the fibrous bands that pull the skin down. Once freed, the skin can lift, and the space fills with new tissue as it heals.
  • Microneedling radiofrequency (MNRF) for boxcar and rolling scars. Very fine needles deliver radiofrequency heat below the surface, which triggers the skin to make new collagen while leaving most of the surface intact.
  • Fractional laser resurfacing, such as fractional CO2 laser, for boxcar scars and uneven texture. The laser treats tiny columns of skin at a time, and the untreated skin between them speeds up healing. It gives more change per session than gentler options but also more downtime.
  • Chemical peels for shallow scars and uneven tone. Superficial and medium-depth peels are repeated every few weeks. Deep peels are generally avoided on brown skin because of the risk of colour changes.
  • Treatments for deep ice pick scars. Very narrow, deep scars sometimes need a targeted technique, such as a focused chemical application or a small punch procedure. Your doctor will explain whether this suits your scars.
  • Treatments for raised scars. Hypertrophic scars and keloids are usually treated with steroid injections into the scar, silicone gel or sheets, or laser, often in combination.

Why combined plans work better

Most faces have a mix of scar types, and each responds to a different method. A typical plan might release rolling scars with subcision first and then smooth the surface over several sessions of microneedling RF or fractional laser, with peels in between to even out skin tone. The American Academy of Dermatology also notes that treatments such as platelet-rich plasma (PRP) are sometimes added to microneedling to improve results. Your doctor will explain the order, the number of sessions and the downtime before you start.

Acne scar treatment for Indian skin

Brown skin heals well, but it reacts to inflammation by making extra pigment. That is why some people notice dark patches after an aggressive peel or laser session. DermNet notes that post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation and keloid scarring are both more common in skin of colour. To lower the risk, your doctor may use gentler settings, allow more time between sessions, prescribe a skin care routine to prepare the skin, and ask you to be strict about sun protection throughout your course.

Aftercare between sessions

  • Wash with a gentle cleanser and apply a plain moisturiser for the first few days.
  • Use a broad-spectrum sunscreen every day and avoid direct sun, especially in the first week after a session.
  • Let any crusts or flakes fall off on their own; do not pick or scrub the skin.
  • Pause retinoids, acids and other active products for as long as your doctor advises, then restart gradually.
  • Contact the clinic if you notice increasing pain, swelling, pus or blistering.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Can acne scars be removed completely?
Most acne scars cannot be erased completely, but they can usually be made much less noticeable. DermNet, the dermatology reference, notes that acne scars are usually permanent, although they may improve with time or with treatment. Your doctor will tell you what level of improvement is realistic for your scar types.
How many acne scar sessions will I need?
Most people need a course of several sessions, spaced a few weeks apart, and some also need subcision or peels in between. Deep or widespread scarring usually needs more sessions than a few shallow scars. The plan is set after your examination and adjusted as your skin responds.
Does acne scar treatment hurt?
Numbing cream is applied before microneedling RF and laser, and a local anaesthetic injection is used for subcision; peels usually sting briefly. Most people feel heat, prickling or pressure during treatment, and the skin can feel sore, like mild sunburn, for a day or two afterwards.
Is acne scar treatment suitable for Indian skin?
Yes, with the right choices. Brown skin is more prone to dark patches after heat-based treatments and strong peels. The American Academy of Dermatology notes that microneedling and radiofrequency microneedling can be used on all skin tones, and laser settings, peel strength and sun protection are adjusted for brown skin to lower the risk.
What is the downtime after a session?
It depends on the treatment. After microneedling RF, most people have redness for one to three days. Fractional laser can cause redness, swelling and fine crusting for about a week, and pinkness can last several weeks, especially in brown skin. Bruising after subcision fades over one to two weeks. Your doctor can plan sessions around work and family events.
Should I treat my acne before my scars?
Yes. New breakouts can leave new scars and make the skin more reactive to procedures, so active acne is controlled first. Tell your doctor about every acne treatment you have used, including isotretinoin tablets, because some scar treatments need a gap after certain medicines.
What is the difference between acne marks and acne scars?
Marks are flat changes in colour, brown or red, that usually fade with time, sun protection and skin care. Scars change the texture of the skin, leaving dents or bumps, and need procedures that rebuild or release the tissue. Many people have both, and they are treated differently; our pigmentation treatment page covers dark marks.
How long do the results of acne scar treatment last?
Improvement from collagen-building treatments usually lasts, because the new collagen stays in the skin. New breakouts can cause new scars, and skin changes with age, so keeping acne under control and protecting your skin from the sun help the result last.
