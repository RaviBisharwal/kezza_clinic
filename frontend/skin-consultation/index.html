Skin Consultation in Jaipur | A Doctor's Assessment & Plan

A skin consultation is a one-to-one visit in which a doctor looks at your skin concern, asks about your health, routine and medicines, explains what is going on and suggests a plan, including how long it may take and what it will cost. It is the right first step for acne, pigmentation, scars, signs of ageing, unwanted hair, moles and skin problems. Book one at our Jaipur, Sikar or Ajmer clinic.

Your Visit

What Happens at a Skin Consultation?

At a skin consultation, a doctor asks about your concern, health, medicines and skin care, examines your skin in good light and sometimes suggests a simple test. You then get an explanation of the likely cause and your options, with the expected number of sessions and costs, before you decide on any treatment.

The doctor starts with your story: when the problem began, what makes it better or worse, the products and medicines you use, including any steroid creams, and your plans such as pregnancy or an upcoming event. Your work and daily routine matter too, because heat, sun and friction affect many skin conditions.

Next comes the examination. Many skin problems can be diagnosed simply by looking closely in good light, sometimes with magnification. The doctor also notes your skin type, which describes how easily your skin burns or tans, because it affects which treatments are safe and at what settings.

Tests are used only when they would change the plan, for example a skin scraping to check for fungus, a patch test for a suspected allergy, or blood tests when acne, hair loss or pigmentation may have a hormonal cause. You leave with an explanation in plain language, what you can do at home, and which clinic treatments, if any, would help.

In Short:

  • Diagnosis first: know the cause before choosing a treatment.
  • Your skin type: guides safe laser and peel settings.
  • Clear plan: sessions, timing and costs explained.

When to Book

Should You Book a Skin Consultation?

A consultation is useful whenever you are unsure what is causing a problem or which treatment is safe for you.

Book a skin consultation if

  • You are not sure what is causing a skin problem, or which treatment would suit you
  • Products from the chemist or salon have not helped, or have made things worse
  • You are planning a laser, peel or injectable treatment and want to know whether it is safe for your skin
  • You have a mole, growth or patch that you would like a doctor to look at

Get urgent medical care instead if

  • You have a fever with a rash, or a rash that does not fade when a glass is pressed on it
  • A rash is spreading fast, blistering, very painful or affects the eyes, mouth or genitals
  • Your face, lips or throat are swelling, or you have difficulty breathing
  • A sore or growth bleeds, crusts or has not healed for more than four weeks: see a doctor promptly

Why your skin type matters

Doctors often describe skin using the Fitzpatrick scale, from type I, which always burns and never tans, to type VI, which rarely burns and tans deeply. Most people in India have types III to V. Darker skin types are more likely to develop post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, the dark marks that can follow acne, eczema, peels or lasers. Knowing your type helps the doctor choose safe peel strengths, laser settings and the gaps between sessions, and explains why a treatment that suits a friend may not be right for you.

The sun protection advice you will hear

Almost every skin plan includes sun protection, because ultraviolet and visible light worsen pigmentation, slow the fading of marks and age the skin. The American Academy of Dermatology recommends a broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher, applied generously and reapplied about every two hours outdoors or after sweating. In Rajasthan's strong sun, shade, a hat and avoiding the hottest hours help too. For melasma, a tinted sunscreen containing iron oxides adds protection from visible light.

Online skin scanner or in-clinic consultation?

Our AI skin scanner is a free way to look at your skin and think about your concerns before a visit. It is not a diagnosis. Research has shown that skin-analysis apps can miss serious problems, including melanoma, so changing moles, unusual spots and rashes always need a doctor's examination. Use the scanner as a starting point, and bring its results to your consultation if you like.

After your consultation

You leave knowing what is likely to be causing your concern and what the options are. For some people the best advice is a simpler routine, stopping a product that is irritating the skin, or no treatment at all. Others start prescription creams, book a course of treatments or have a test first. If a clinic treatment is suggested, you hear how many sessions it usually takes, how far apart they are, what recovery to expect and the total cost, so you can decide without pressure. Follow-up visits check progress and adjust the plan.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an appointment for a skin consultation?
Booking ahead is best, so that a doctor has time for your visit. Message us on WhatsApp or call with your concern and preferred clinic, and our team will suggest a time. All three clinics are open from 9 AM to 8 PM, seven days a week.
What should I bring to my skin consultation?
Bring a list of your skin care products, makeup, supplements and medicines, including any creams prescribed or bought over the counter, and clear photos of flares or of how a spot has changed. Previous reports or prescriptions are also helpful.
Can I start treatment on the same day?
Sometimes. Simple treatments can occasionally begin the same day if they suit your skin. Many treatments, such as peels and lasers, work better and more safely after a few weeks of skin preparation, a patch test or tests, so the doctor will advise the right time to start.
Can an app or AI scanner diagnose my skin?
No. Online tools can help you notice changes, but they cannot replace an examination. A review in the BMJ found that current smartphone apps cannot be relied on to detect all skin cancers. Our AI skin scanner is a starting point, and a doctor confirms the diagnosis.
Why does my skin type matter for lasers and peels?
Skin type describes how your skin reacts to the sun, from types that always burn to types that rarely burn and tan deeply. Medium and darker skin types are more prone to dark marks after lasers, peels or irritation, so the doctor chooses gentler settings, prepares the skin and spaces sessions with this in mind.
Do I need sunscreen if I have brown skin?
Yes. Sunscreen is for everyone. Use a broad-spectrum sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher and reapply about every two hours outdoors or after sweating. A tinted sunscreen with iron oxides also helps people with melasma or dark marks.
Will I need any tests?
Most skin problems are diagnosed by examination alone. Tests such as skin scrapings for fungus, patch tests for allergy or blood tests are suggested only when they would help, and the doctor explains why and what they cost first.
Can I ask about a hair problem at the same visit?
Yes, you can mention it. For hair fall and hair thinning, our hair loss consultation includes a scalp assessment, and the doctor can advise whether a separate hair visit would be useful.
