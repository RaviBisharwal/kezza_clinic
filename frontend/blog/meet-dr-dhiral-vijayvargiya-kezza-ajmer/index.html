Dr. Dhiral's Surgical Background

Dr. Dhiral Vijayvargiya's journey into hair transplant surgery is unusual — and that is precisely what sets her apart. Most hair transplant surgeons come from a dermatology background. Dr. Dhiral trained as an Oral & Maxillofacial (OMF) Surgeon, earning her MDS from a Rajasthan university after her BDS degree, and is registered with the Rajasthan State Dental Council (Reg. No. A-8769).

Maxillofacial surgery trains surgeons to operate within millimetres of the trigeminal nerve, the facial artery, and the intricate anatomy of the orbital and nasal regions. This demands a level of anatomical precision that directly benefits two critical phases of a hair transplant: designing a natural-looking hairline that follows the face's proportions, and making precise incisions (slits) for graft implantation without damaging existing follicles.

At Kezza Ajmer, Dr. Dhiral applies that hospital-grade surgical discipline to help patients restore hair density, diagnose the cause of their hair loss, and approach facial aesthetics — all in a clinical environment that feels calm rather than clinical.

Hair Transplant: How Dr. Dhiral Explains It to Patients

Many patients arrive at the clinic having read conflicting information online. Dr. Dhiral's approach is to explain the procedure in plain language, step by step, so patients feel informed and comfortable before committing to anything.

How Dr. Dhiral typically explains FUE to a new patient:

"Your scalp has a permanent donor zone at the back and sides — these follicles are genetically resistant to the hormone DHT that causes male-pattern baldness. What we do in FUE is take individual follicular units from that zone, one by one, using a tiny punch that leaves no linear scar. We then make micro-incisions in the recipient area — the top or front of your head — and implant the grafts at the correct angle and density."

"The whole procedure is done under local anaesthesia. You will feel some initial injections; after that the scalp is numb and you can watch something on your phone or rest. A typical session for 1,500–2,000 grafts takes 5–7 hours."

"The transplanted grafts shed after 2–4 weeks — this is normal and expected. New hair starts to grow from month 3, and the final result is visible at 12–14 months. The hair that grows is permanent."

Real Patient Conversations

The following excerpts are representative of the kinds of questions Dr. Dhiral routinely answers during consultations. They are based on common patient queries at Kezza Ajmer; names are anonymised.

Patient Conversation 1 — "Am I a good candidate?"

Rahul, 32, Ajmer: "Doctor, I'm losing hair at the crown. I've been told I need 2,000 grafts. Is that accurate? And will the result look natural?"

Dr. Dhiral: "I can't give you a number without seeing your scalp under trichoscopy. Graft counts depend on the size of the bald area, the density of your donor zone, and the calibre of each follicular unit. What I can tell you is this: we only transplant as many grafts as your donor zone can safely provide. Overharvesting weakens the donor area and compromises future sessions. As for naturalness — that comes from the angle and direction of each incision, which is designed to mimic how your hair originally grew. I design the hairline to suit your face, not a generic template."

Patient Conversation 2 — "Will hair loss continue after the transplant?"

Priya, 28, Beawar: "My mother had hair thinning too. If I get a transplant, will my existing hair keep falling?"

Dr. Dhiral: "Yes — the transplanted hair is permanent because it comes from DHT-resistant follicles. But your existing native hair can continue to thin if the underlying cause (usually androgenetic alopecia) is not managed. That is why I typically recommend a maintenance plan alongside the transplant: either oral finasteride, topical minoxidil, or regular PRP sessions. This protects the native hair you still have and maximises the long-term result. We will decide together which option suits you based on your history."

Patient Conversation 3 — "I just want to look younger, not operated"

Sunita, 41, Ajmer: "I have deep forehead lines and my cheeks look hollow. I don't want surgery. Is there anything that can help without making me look frozen or fake?"

Dr. Dhiral: "That's the most common concern I hear, and it's completely valid. Botox for forehead lines works by relaxing the specific muscle causing the crease — if the dosage is calibrated correctly, expression is preserved and there is no frozen look. For the hollow cheeks, a small amount of hyaluronic acid filler can restore volume without adding puffiness. I start conservatively — less is always more — and the result is reviewed after two weeks. You can always add more; you cannot take away too much. My maxillofacial training helps me understand exactly where the facial vessels and nerves run, which makes these injections safer."

Patient Conversation 4 — "How long is the recovery?"

Vikram, 36, Pushkar: "I run a business and meet clients every day. How many days will I need to hide at home after the transplant?"

Dr. Dhiral: "Honest answer: about 7–10 days before the scalp looks presentable in a business setting. The first 2 days there is mild swelling and redness. By day 5 the tiny crusts begin to shed. By day 10 most patients can return to meetings wearing a hat if needed — and many do. The implanted area will look pink for a few more weeks but it is easily covered. I will send you home with a full written aftercare sheet, and I am always available on WhatsApp for any question in the first two weeks."

What to Expect at Your First Consultation with Dr. Dhiral

A first consultation at Kezza Ajmer with Dr. Dhiral typically runs 30–45 minutes and follows this structure:

  1. History and goals — how long has the hair loss been happening, family history, any medications, and what outcome the patient is hoping for.
  2. Trichoscopy examination — a scalp magnification device (trichoscope) is used to assess miniaturisation of follicles, donor density, and scalp health.
  3. Norwood staging — the extent of hair loss is mapped to a standard scale so options can be discussed clearly.
  4. Treatment options explained — Dr. Dhiral presents the options that are medically appropriate: surgery, PRP/GFC, medical management, or a combination.
  5. Honest recommendation — if a patient is not a suitable candidate for transplant yet, Dr. Dhiral says so plainly. There is no pressure to commit on the same day.
  6. Questions and next steps — patients are encouraged to take notes, ask every question, and discuss with family before deciding.

Is hair transplant painful at Kezza Ajmer?
Dr. Dhiral uses a tumescent local anaesthetic technique. Patients feel a brief sting from the initial injections; after that the scalp is fully numb and the procedure is comfortable. Most patients describe a mild pressure sensation rather than pain and use the time to rest or watch something on their phone.
How many grafts do I need for a hair transplant?
Graft count depends on the size of the thinning area, the density of your donor zone, and your long-term hair loss trajectory. A trichoscopy examination at the Ajmer clinic gives an accurate estimate. Typical full-crown sessions range from 1,500 to 3,500 grafts; Dr. Dhiral never recommends harvesting more than your donor zone can safely provide.
Why does Dr. Dhiral's maxillofacial background matter for facial aesthetics?
Oral and Maxillofacial surgeons train extensively in the anatomy of the face — nerves, arteries, muscles and bone. This knowledge directly improves the safety and precision of filler injections (avoiding vascular structures) and Botox placement (targeting the exact muscle responsible for a line without over-relaxing adjacent muscles). It is the same anatomical understanding that makes hairline design and graft incisions precise.
