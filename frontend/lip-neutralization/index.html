The Treatment

What is lip neutralization?

Lips can be naturally dark, purple-toned or two-toned because of genetics and the natural pigment in the skin, and can also darken with sun exposure, smoking, frequent lip-licking and dryness. Lip neutralization does not bleach the lips. It uses colour correction: warm pigments such as peach, coral or orange are implanted to cancel out cool, bluish-brown tones, so the lips look lighter and more even.

The pigment is placed in the upper layer of the lip skin with a fine permanent-makeup needle, after numbing. Dark lips usually absorb and hold colour unevenly, so the colour is built up gradually over 3 sessions, 21 days apart, which lets each layer heal before the next.

Once the darkness is neutralised, some people choose a soft lip tint on top — see lip blush on our permanent makeup page. Like all permanent makeup, the colour fades gradually over the years and can be refreshed.

Lip neutralization changes how the lips look, not the cause of the darkness. Smoking, sun exposure and lip-licking can make the colour fade faster. A sudden change in lip colour, or dark spots that appear quickly, should be checked by a doctor before any PMU.

Suitability

Is lip neutralization right for you?

It may suit you if

  • Your lips are naturally dark or uneven in colour
  • Your lip skin is healthy, without cracks or infection
  • You do not smoke, or have stopped
  • You want a gradual, natural-looking change over a few sessions

Tell us first if

  • You get cold sores (herpes) on or around the lips
  • You have had lip fillers or lip surgery recently
  • You are pregnant or breastfeeding — PMU is usually postponed
  • You take isotretinoin or blood thinners, or have diabetes
  • You tend to form keloids, or have allergies to pigments or numbing creams

Lip darkness that appears suddenly, or patches inside the mouth, can have medical causes and should be checked by a doctor first.

Step by Step

How your lip neutralization sessions work

  1. 01Consultation

    Tone and patch test

    Krishna looks at your lip colour and undertone, asks about cold sores, fillers and medicines, and may do a patch test.

  2. 02Colour plan

    Choosing the corrector

    A warm correcting shade is chosen for your lips and skin tone.

  3. 03Numbing

    Comfort first

    A numbing cream is applied before the pigment work begins.

  4. 04Session 1

    First layer

    Pigment is placed evenly across the lips; they look brighter and swollen at first.

  5. 05Day 21

    Session 2

    The colour is assessed after healing and a second layer is added.

  6. 06Day 42

    Session 3

    The final layer evens out the tone; the healed result shows about 4 to 6 weeks later.

Healing

Healing after each session

  1. Days 1–2

    Swollen, bright

    Lips feel tender and look swollen and more intense in colour.

  2. Days 3–7

    Flaking

    Lips peel and flake; colour can look patchy. Do not pick.

  3. Days 7–14

    Colour settles

    The colour may look faded before it returns gradually.

  4. Day 21

    Next session

    The next layer is added once the lips have healed.

  5. 4–6 weeks

    Healed result

    The final colour is visible after the last session heals.

Safety

Risks and how they are reduced

Common and temporary

  • Swelling and tenderness for one to two days
  • Dryness, flaking and patchy colour while the lips heal
  • Colour that looks too bright at first, then softens

Less common

  • A cold sore flare in people who get them
  • Infection if aftercare is not followed
  • An allergic reaction to pigment (rare); a patch test helps
  • Uneven colour that needs another session
  • Changes in colour over time, or pigment spreading slightly beyond the lip line

Contact the clinic if your lips become very swollen, painful or hot, develop blisters or pus, or if you have a fever after a session. Tell your radiographer about permanent makeup before an MRI scan.
