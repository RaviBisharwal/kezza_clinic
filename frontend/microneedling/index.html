The Treatment

What is microneedling?

Microneedling works with your skin's natural healing. The device's fine needles create tiny, evenly spaced micro-injuries. The skin responds the way it does to any small wound: it calms inflammation, then builds new collagen and elastin over the following weeks, and finally remodels it. The result is skin that can look smoother and firmer, with softer scars.

The needle depth is adjusted for each area — shallower on thin skin around the eyes and forehead, deeper over acne scars on the cheeks. Numbing cream is applied first, and a soothing serum such as hyaluronic acid is often used during or after the session.

Because collagen builds slowly, results appear gradually. Many people notice smoother, brighter skin within a couple of weeks; scar improvement is best judged 6 to 12 weeks after the last session of a course. A course of at least 3 sessions, 21 days apart is usual.

Some clinics also offer radiofrequency (RF) microneedling, which adds heat through the needles. Ask your doctor which method your plan uses and whether the price is different. Deep, narrow 'ice-pick' scars often need other techniques too — see scar revision.

Suitability

Is microneedling right for you?

Microneedling may suit you if

  • You have acne scars, but your acne is now under control
  • You want smoother texture or less visible pores
  • You have darker skin and want a lower-risk option than resurfacing lasers
  • You can commit to a course of sessions 21 days apart

Tell your doctor first if

  • You have active acne, a skin infection or cold sores in the area
  • You have eczema, psoriasis or rosacea on the face
  • You tend to form keloids or raised scars
  • You take isotretinoin or blood thinners, or have a bleeding disorder
  • You are pregnant, have uncontrolled diabetes, or a mole or spot that is changing

If you still get new pimples, treating active acne comes first — needling over active acne can spread infection and inflammation. See our acne treatment page.

Step by Step

What happens during a microneedling session

  1. 01Consultation

    Skin assessment

    Your doctor examines your skin and scars, checks your medicines and history, and agrees the plan.

  2. 02Numbing

    30–45 minutes

    A numbing cream is applied so the treatment is comfortable.

  3. 03Treatment

    About 20–30 minutes

    The device passes over the skin in sections, with the depth set for each area. Pinpoint bleeding is normal.

  4. 04Soothing

    Calm the skin

    A soothing serum or mask is applied, and you are given aftercare advice.

  5. 05Aftercare

    First 48 hours

    No makeup for 24 hours; gentle cleanser, moisturiser and sunscreen; avoid gym, sauna and active products.

  6. 06Next session

    21 days later

    Sessions are repeated every 21 days, then results are reviewed.

Safety

Side effects and safety

Expected and short-lived

  • Redness, warmth and mild swelling for 1 to 3 days
  • Pinpoint bleeding during the treatment
  • Dryness, tightness or light flaking

Less common

  • Breakouts or small white bumps (milia)
  • Dark marks (post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation)
  • Infection or a cold sore flare
  • Allergic reaction to a serum or numbing cream
  • Track marks if the device is used incorrectly

Contact the clinic if the treated skin becomes increasingly painful, swollen or hot, oozes, or if you develop a fever.
