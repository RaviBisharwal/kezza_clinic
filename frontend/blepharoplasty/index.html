The Procedure

What is blepharoplasty?

Blepharoplasty is cosmetic surgery to change the look of hooded or drooping eyelids or bags under the eyes. As we age, eyelid skin stretches and the fat that cushions the eye can bulge forward, which can make you look tired even when you are not.

In an upper blepharoplasty, the surgeon makes a cut in the natural crease of the upper lid and removes excess skin, sometimes with a little muscle or fat. The scar sits in the crease and is usually well hidden. In a lower blepharoplasty, the cut is made just below the lashes or on the inside of the lower lid, and fat is removed or repositioned to smooth the bags; loose skin can be tightened at the same time.

The NHS describes eyelid surgery as taking around 45 minutes to 2 and a half hours, under local or general anaesthetic, and most people go home the same day. When very heavy upper lids hang over the lashes and block the upper part of your vision, surgery can also improve your field of view.

Eyelid surgery has limits. It does not remove dark circles caused by pigment, lines at the corners of the eyes, or a low eyebrow — and a droopy lid caused by a weak lifting muscle (ptosis) needs a different operation. Your assessment makes sure the right problem is treated.

Options

Types of eyelid surgery

Your surgeon chooses the technique after examining your eyelids.

Upper eyelid surgery

Removes excess skin (and sometimes a strip of muscle and fat) through a cut in the upper eyelid crease, for hooded lids or lids that touch the lashes.

Lower eyelid surgery

Removes or repositions the fat that causes under-eye bags, through a cut just below the lashes; loose skin can be tightened.

Inside-the-lid approach

For some lower-lid bags, fat is reached through the inside of the lower lid (transconjunctival), so there is no visible skin scar.

Upper and lower together

All four eyelids can be treated in one operation when both upper hooding and lower bags are a concern.

Suitability

Is eyelid surgery right for you?

Blepharoplasty may suit you if

  • Hooded upper lids make you look tired or older, or sit on your lashes
  • Puffy bags under your eyes are caused by fat, not just fluid or pigment
  • You are in good general health and do not smoke, or can stop
  • You want a refreshed look, not a change to the shape of your eyes

Tell your surgeon first if

  • You have dry eyes, glaucoma or thyroid eye disease
  • You have had laser eye surgery or other eye surgery recently
  • You take blood thinners, aspirin or supplements that affect bleeding
  • You have high blood pressure, diabetes or a bleeding disorder
  • Your problem is mainly dark pigment or a low eyebrow — another treatment may suit you better

You may be asked to have your eyesight or eye health checked before surgery, so that your eyelids are planned safely.

Safety

Risks and side effects of blepharoplasty

Common and usually temporary

  • Swelling and bruising around the eyes
  • Watery, dry, gritty or sensitive eyes for a few weeks
  • Blurred vision for a short time, often from ointment
  • Tightness along the scars for a few months

Less common or rare

  • Uneven eyelids, noticeable scarring or changed feeling in the eyelids
  • Difficulty closing the eyes fully, or the lower lid pulling down or outwards (ectropion)
  • Double vision or injury to the eye muscles (rare)
  • Bleeding into the eye socket, which can affect vision (rare)
  • General surgical risks such as infection, bleeding or a reaction to the anaesthetic; the result may not be what you hoped and further surgery may be needed

Seek urgent medical help if you have severe pain around the eye, a sudden change or loss of vision, or rapidly increasing swelling after eyelid surgery.
