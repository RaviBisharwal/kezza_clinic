GFC Hair Treatment in Jaipur | Growth Factor Concentrate

GFC therapy uses growth factors prepared from a small sample of your own blood to support thinning hair follicles. Unlike PRP, the growth factors are released before injection and the cells are left out, which many patients find more comfortable. It works best on hair that is thinning but still growing, alongside medical treatment.

The Treatment

What Is GFC Hair Treatment?

GFC stands for growth factor concentrate. Like PRP, it starts with a small amount of your own blood. The sample is prepared with a dedicated kit in which the platelets are activated, so they release their growth factors before injection. This cell-free (acellular) concentrate is then injected into the thinning areas of the scalp.

Growth factors are proteins that help regulate the hair growth cycle. They are thought to support weakened follicles, prolong the growth phase and improve the blood supply around the root. That is why GFC is used for hair that is thinning but still growing, not for areas that have been smooth and bald for years.

Because the injected fluid contains no cells, many patients find GFC injections less painful than PRP. In a small randomised trial published in 2026, GFC and PRP gave similar hair improvement, while pain scores were clearly lower with GFC.

In Short:

  • From your own blood: a small sample is prepared for each session.
  • Cell-free concentrate: growth factors are released before injection.
  • No downtime: most people return to their day straight away.

Suitability

Who Is GFC Suitable For?

GFC helps follicles that are weak but still alive, so a doctor's diagnosis comes first.

GFC may suit you if

  • You have early to moderate male or female pattern hair thinning
  • You are shedding more hair, or your parting or crown is widening
  • You want to protect native hair before or after a hair transplant
  • You found PRP injections uncomfortable, or would prefer fewer sessions

Check with your doctor first if

  • You have a bleeding or platelet disorder, or take blood-thinning medicines
  • You have an active scalp infection, or a condition such as lupus or thyroid disease that is not under control
  • Your hair fall is caused by low iron, a thyroid problem or another condition that needs its own treatment
  • The area has been completely bald for a long time; GFC cannot create new follicles

A hair loss consultation with trichoscopy tells you whether GFC, PRP, medicine or a transplant is the best first step for you.

Procedure

How a GFC Session Works

From blood sample to injections, a session is completed in a single visit.

01

Scalp Assessment

Trichoscopy checks density, miniaturisation and the pattern of thinning, and other causes of hair fall are ruled out.

02

Blood Sample

A small amount of blood is drawn from your arm, as in a routine blood test.

03

GFC Preparation

The sample is processed with a GFC kit and a centrifuge so the platelets release their growth factors, and the cells are separated out.

04

Numbing & Injections

After numbing, the concentrate is injected into the thinning areas through very fine needles.

05

Aftercare

You can usually return to your day straight away, with simple instructions on washing and hair products for the next day or two.

GFC vs PRP: what is the difference?

Both treatments deliver growth factors from your own blood to thinning follicles. The difference is in the preparation and in what is injected.

GFCPRP
What is injectedGrowth factors released from platelets, without the cellsConcentrated platelets in plasma
PreparationKit-based preparation with platelet activationCentrifuge, often with a double spin
ComfortUsually rated less painfulModerate stinging after numbing
Typical course at Kezza3 to 4 sessions, about 4 weeks apart4 to 6 sessions, 3 to 4 weeks apart
EvidenceNewer, smaller but growing evidenceMore studies over a longer period

What does the research say?

A randomised controlled trial published in 2026 in the Journal of Cutaneous and Aesthetic Surgery compared GFC with PRP in 44 people with pattern hair loss who were also using 5% minoxidil. After three monthly sessions, hair improvement was similar in both groups. The clear difference was comfort: the median pain score was 2 out of 10 with GFC and 6 out of 10 with PRP, and satisfaction was higher with GFC. The authors note that the study was small and the follow-up short.

A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis in Aesthetic Plastic Surgery, covering 12 studies and 745 patients, found that growth-factor injections appear safe and improve hair density, thickness and patient satisfaction over 12 months. It also warned that the studies used different methods and several had a high risk of bias, so the evidence is promising rather than settled.

GFC works best as part of a plan

GFC is one part of hair-loss treatment, not a replacement for a diagnosis. Your doctor may combine it with medicines such as minoxidil or finasteride, check for problems like low iron or thyroid imbalance, and suggest changes to scalp care. If some areas have already gone bald, a hair transplant can restore them, while GFC helps protect the hair around them.

Side effects and aftercare

Most people have only mild tenderness, redness or slight swelling for a day. Small bruises at injection sites are possible. On the day of the session, avoid touching the scalp more than necessary and follow your doctor's advice on washing, hair products and exercise. Tell your doctor in advance about any blood-thinning medicines or bleeding problems.

What affects the cost of GFC?

GFC kits usually cost more per session than PRP, while PRP may need more sessions, so it is worth comparing the whole course rather than a single session. The number of sessions you need depends on your stage of hair loss and how you respond. Your doctor recommends a plan after examining your scalp.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Is GFC better than PRP?
Current evidence suggests the two give similar regrowth. GFC is usually more comfortable and may need fewer sessions, while PRP has a longer research history. Read our PRP vs GFC comparison.
How many GFC sessions will I need?
Most people start with 3 to 4 sessions about 4 weeks apart, followed by maintenance every few months. Your doctor adjusts the plan to your hair loss and your response.
Is GFC painful?
The scalp is numbed first. Because the injected fluid contains no cells, most people feel less stinging than with PRP, and any tenderness usually settles within a day.
When will I see results from GFC?
Hair grows slowly. Many people first notice less hair fall after 2 to 3 months, and changes in thickness are easier to see at 4 to 6 months in progress photos.
Can GFC regrow hair on a bald area?
No. GFC supports follicles that are thinning but still alive. Areas that have been smooth and bald for a long time have no active follicles left, and a hair transplant is the option there.
Is GFC safe?
GFC is prepared from your own blood, so reactions to the concentrate itself are unlikely. Possible side effects include tenderness, redness, mild swelling and small bruises at the injection sites. It should always be done with sterile technique by trained medical staff.
Can I have GFC after a hair transplant?
Yes. Doctors often use growth factor therapy alongside a transplant to support the native hair around the transplanted area. Your doctor will advise the right timing.
Can women have GFC treatment?
Yes. GFC is used for female pattern thinning, such as a widening parting. Women with diffuse hair fall are first checked for causes such as thyroid problems, low iron or hormonal changes.
Is GFC available at all Kezza clinics?
GFC is available at our Jaipur and Sikar clinics. Our Ajmer clinic offers hair-loss diagnosis and consultation and can guide you on the next step.
