How It Works

What Is Laser Hair Removal?

Laser hair removal works on a principle called selective photothermolysis. Light of a specific wavelength is absorbed by melanin, the pigment in dark hair. In the hair shaft and follicle, that light turns into heat, which damages the follicle's ability to grow a new hair, while cooling protects the surrounding skin.

Different wavelengths reach different depths. A triple-wavelength system combines 755 nm (alexandrite), 808 nm (diode) and 1064 nm (Nd:YAG) light. The longer 1064 nm wavelength is generally considered gentler on darker skin, because less of its energy is absorbed by the skin's own pigment. The settings are adjusted to your skin type and hair.

Laser treats many hairs at once, so it suits large areas such as the legs, arms, back and underarms, as well as the face. It gives a long-term reduction rather than a promise that every hair will go: most people need a series of sessions, and any hair that grows back is usually finer and lighter.

In Short:

  • Targets dark hair: the light is absorbed by pigment in the hair.
  • A series of sessions: hair grows in cycles, so several sessions are needed.
  • Long-term reduction: any regrowth is usually finer and lighter.

Suitability

Is Laser Hair Removal Right for You?

Laser works best on dark hair. Your skin type and hair colour decide the settings and the likely result.

Laser usually works well if

  • Your unwanted hair is dark brown or black
  • You want less hair on the face, underarms, arms, legs, back, chest or bikini line
  • You get ingrown hairs or irritation from shaving or waxing
  • You can avoid sun tanning before and during your course

Laser may not be right if

  • Your hair is white, grey, blonde or red: laser has too little pigment to target, so electrolysis is used instead
  • Your skin is freshly tanned or sunburnt, or has an infection or rash in the area
  • You are pregnant; treatment is usually postponed
  • You take medicines that make the skin sensitive to light, or you form keloid scars; tell the team first

Hormonal conditions such as PCOS can cause extra facial or body hair. Laser still helps, but you may need more sessions and occasional maintenance, and the cause should be checked by a doctor.

Procedure

What Happens at a Laser Session

Your first visit includes a skin check and a plan for your areas and sessions.

01

Consultation & Skin Check

The team checks your skin type, hair colour and medical history, and plans the areas and number of sessions with you.

02

Preparation

You shave the area the day before (do not wax or pluck). The skin is cleaned, and everyone in the room wears protective eyewear.

03

Test Patch

A small area may be tested first to confirm the right settings for your skin.

04

Laser Treatment

The handpiece is moved over the area in passes while the cooling system protects the skin. Most people feel warmth or a quick snapping sensation.

05

Aftercare

Mild redness settles within hours. Use sunscreen, avoid sun and heat for a few days, and do not pluck or wax between sessions.

Laser hair removal for Indian skin

Laser hair removal works best when there is a clear contrast between dark hair and lighter skin, because the laser is aimed at pigment. Indian skin also contains more pigment, so the skin can absorb some of the energy. That does not rule laser out, but it means the choice of wavelength and settings matters. The American Academy of Dermatology notes that laser hair removal can be used on darker skin tones but must be performed very carefully.

Longer wavelengths such as 1064 nm Nd:YAG pass through the skin's surface pigment more easily and are generally considered the safer option for darker skin. A triple-wavelength laser lets the team combine wavelengths and adjust energy for your skin type and the thickness of your hair, while contact cooling protects the surface. Staying out of the sun before and during your course lowers the risk of burns and pigment changes.

Areas we treat

  • Face: upper lip, chin, jawline, sideburns and cheeks.
  • Underarms: one of the most popular areas, with quick sessions.
  • Arms and legs: full or half arms and legs.
  • Back, chest and shoulders: commonly treated in men.
  • Bikini line: treated with extra care for comfort and privacy.

How to prepare for laser hair removal

  • Avoid sun tanning and use a broad-spectrum sunscreen (SPF 30 or higher) on exposed areas.
  • Stop waxing, plucking, threading and electrolysis on the area for about 4 weeks before treatment, because laser needs the hair root in place. Shaving is fine.
  • Shave the area the day before your appointment.
  • Come with clean skin, without creams, deodorant or makeup on the area.
  • Tell the team about any medicines, recent sun exposure or skin conditions.

Side effects and safety

Mild redness, swelling and a warm feeling are common and usually fade within a few hours. Uncommon side effects include blistering, crusting, scarring and changes in skin colour or texture, which is why the right settings, eye protection and a trained team are essential. Follow your aftercare advice: avoid sun, hot baths and saunas for a few days, and use sunscreen on treated skin.

Why laser cannot remove white hair

White and grey hairs have little or no melanin, so the laser has nothing to target and the follicle is not damaged. This is true for every laser type. For white, grey, blonde or red hair, electrolysis treats each follicle directly, whatever its colour. If you have a mix of dark and white hairs, laser can treat the dark hairs and electrolysis the rest. Read more in our guide Why laser can't remove white hair, or see white hair removal.

The cost depends on the size of the area, the number of sessions you need and whether you treat one area or several. Many people choose a package for an area or a combination of areas. Ask the team on WhatsApp for the current options after your skin check.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does full body laser hair removal cost?
At Kezza Clinic, full body laser hair removal costs ₹4,000 per session, usually about 10 sessions. See our full body laser hair removal page for details.
How many laser hair removal sessions will I need?
Most people need 6 to 8 sessions, spaced several weeks apart, because only hairs in the active growth phase respond at each session. Hormonal hair growth, for example with PCOS, may need more sessions and occasional maintenance.
Is laser hair removal permanent?
It gives a long-term reduction rather than a guarantee that every hair is gone. Many people stay hair-reduced for months or years; any hair that grows back is usually finer and lighter, and can be treated with maintenance sessions.
Is laser hair removal safe for Indian skin?
Yes, when the right laser and settings are used. Longer wavelengths such as 1064 nm Nd:YAG are gentler on darker skin, and a cooling system protects the surface. Avoid tanning before sessions, because tanned skin is more likely to react.
Does laser hair removal hurt?
Most people feel warmth or a quick snapping sensation, like a rubber band against the skin. Built-in cooling makes it more comfortable, and sensitive areas may feel a little stronger.
What should I do before a session?
Avoid sun tanning, stop waxing, plucking and threading for about 4 weeks before (shaving is fine), and shave the area the day before. Tell the team about any medicines, as some make the skin more sensitive to light.
Can laser remove white or grey hair?
No. Laser needs dark pigment in the hair to work. White, grey, blonde and red hairs are treated with electrolysis. Read why laser cannot remove white hair.
Are there side effects?
Redness, mild swelling and warmth are common and usually settle within hours to a day. Blistering, crusting or pigment changes are uncommon and are more likely on tanned skin or with the wrong settings.
Can men have laser hair removal?
Yes. Men commonly treat the back, chest, shoulders and neck, or shape the beard line and cheeks.
Is laser hair removal available in Sikar?
Yes. It is offered at our Sikar clinic on Silver Jubilee Road, opposite S.K. Hospital, as well as at our Jaipur clinic in Khatipura.
