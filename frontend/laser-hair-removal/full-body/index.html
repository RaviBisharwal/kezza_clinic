The Treatment

How full body laser hair removal works

Laser hair removal works by selective photothermolysis: the laser's light is absorbed by the dark pigment (melanin) in the hair, turns into heat and damages the follicle that grows it, while the skin around it is cooled and protected. Treated hairs shed over the following one to three weeks.

Hair grows in cycles, and the laser only affects follicles that are in the active growth phase at the time of treatment. That is why a course of sessions, spaced weeks apart, is needed to catch each follicle when it is growing — and why most full-body plans run to about 10 sessions.

Laser reduces hair rather than removing every hair for good. Most people see a large, long-lasting reduction, with remaining hairs growing finer and lighter; occasional maintenance sessions may be needed, especially when hormones drive hair growth, as in PCOS.

This page covers full-body plans. For single areas such as the face, underarms or bikini line, and for how the laser works on Indian skin, see our main laser hair removal page.

Suitability

Is full body laser right for you?

Full body laser may suit you if

  • You have dark hair on several areas and want a long-term reduction
  • You are tired of waxing, ingrown hairs or shaving rash
  • You are a woman or man with any skin tone, treated with suitable settings
  • You can avoid sun tanning and keep to a session every few weeks

Tell us first if

  • Your hair is white, grey, blonde or red — laser will not work; white hair removal uses electrolysis
  • You have a fresh tan, sunburn or use self-tan
  • You are pregnant — treatment is usually postponed
  • You take isotretinoin or medicines that make skin sensitive to light
  • You have tattoos, keloids or a skin infection in the area

Sudden or excessive hair growth in women can be a sign of a hormone problem such as PCOS. It is worth having this checked by a doctor alongside laser treatment.

Step by Step

Your full body laser sessions

  1. 01Consultation

    Skin and hair check

    Your skin type, hair colour, medicines and medical history are reviewed, and the areas in your plan are agreed.

  2. 02Test patch

    When needed

    A small area is treated first to check how your skin responds to the settings.

  3. 03Before each visit

    Shave, don't wax

    Shave the areas the day before. Do not wax, thread or pluck for several weeks, and avoid tanning.

  4. 04Session

    Cooling and laser

    You wear eye protection; the laser glides over each area with cooling. Most people feel a quick warm snap.

  5. 05Aftercare

    24–48 hours

    Use sunscreen, avoid sun, saunas and hard workouts for a day or two, and moisturise if the skin feels dry.

  6. 06Repeat

    Every 4–8 weeks

    Sessions are repeated as new hairs enter the growth phase — usually about 10 in total.

Safety

Side effects and safety

Common and short-lived

  • Redness and small bumps around the follicles for a few hours
  • Mild itching, warmth or tingling
  • Temporary dryness of the treated skin

Less common

  • Burns or blisters, especially on tanned skin
  • Darker or lighter patches of skin, usually temporary
  • Rarely, increased hair growth near treated areas (paradoxical growth)
  • Eye injury if eye protection is not used — shields are always worn

Contact the clinic if you notice blisters, crusting or a burn-like patch after a session.
