Clinical Fundamentals

What is a Hair Transplant?

A hair transplant is an autologous outpatient surgical procedure that moves active follicular units from your permanent donor area (the back and sides of your scalp) to areas showing thinning or recession. Because donor follicles are genetically resistant to dihydrotestosterone (DHT), they retain their growth characteristics permanently once placed into their new location.

At Kezza Clinic, our doctors specialize in Follicular Unit Extraction (FUE), Follicular Unit Transplantation (FUT), and Direct Hair Implantation (DHI). We assess your donor density and scalp laxity to select the surgical approach that best supports natural angle, depth, and cosmetic density.

In Short:

  • Autologous Roots: Uses your own live follicles with zero risk of foreign rejection.
  • Permanent Characteristic: DHT-resistant donor roots continue growing naturally for life.
  • Versatile Application: Restores scalp crowns, frontal hairlines, beards, and eyebrows.
Assessment

Am I a Candidate for Hair Restoration?

Ideal Candidates

  • Men with male pattern hair loss (Norwood 2 to 6).
  • Women with stable frontal hairline thinning.
  • Patchy facial hair needing beard or eyebrow density.
  • Scalp scarring from trauma, burns, or past surgery.
  • Realistic expectations matching donor hair supply.
Stabilized shedding ensures durable hairline design.

When Surgery is Not Advised

  • Diffuse unpatterned alopecia with weak donor zones.
  • Active autoimmune conditions like untreated alopecia areata.
  • Expecting dense teenage hairlines with limited donor roots.
  • Acute temporary shedding from illness or nutritional gaps.
PRP and GFC therapy are preferred for temporary hair fall.

Realistic Milestones

Results & Recovery Timeline

Hair restoration requires biological time. Here is what to expect at each stage.

W1

Week 1: Settling & Crust Formation

Mild crusts form around grafts and rinse clean by day 7 to 10 with gentle washing.

M1

Month 1: Normal Shedding Phase

Transplanted hair shafts shed between weeks 2 and 6. Roots remain securely seated.

M3

Month 3: Early Regrowth

Fine, delicate new hairs begin sprouting through the scalp surface.

M6

Month 6: Visible Density

Noticeable density emerges as hair shafts thicken and blend naturally.

M12

Month 12: Final Maturation

Full follicular maturation is achieved, providing lasting aesthetic density.

Medical Note: Results vary from person to person and depend on your hair type, cause of hair loss and how closely aftercare is followed. This information is for general awareness and is not a substitute for a medical consultation.
Patient Safety

Downtime, Aftercare & Safety

Following these clinical protocols protects your grafts during early healing.

Recommended Do's

  • Sleep with head elevated at 45 degrees for 3 nights.
  • Mist recipient area with saline every few hours.
  • Wash gently using clinic technique beginning on day 3.
  • Take prescribed medications exactly as directed.
  • Attend day-10 review for professional scabbing clearance.

Important Don'ts

  • Do not scratch, pick, or rub recipient crusts.
  • Avoid strenuous workouts and sweating for 14 days.
  • Shield scalp from direct sun and chlorinated pools.
  • Avoid alcohol and smoking for 7 days post-procedure.
  • Avoid tight caps or helmets causing graft friction.

Expected mild donor tightness and slight forehead swelling settle naturally within 4 to 7 days.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does the procedure take?
Typically 4 to 8 hours. Conducted as day-care treatment under local numbing; you return home the same day.
Is transplanted hair permanent?
Yes. Grafts taken from DHT-resistant donor areas continue growing permanently.
Why does transplanted hair shed initially?
Shedding between weeks 2 and 6 is normal shock loss. Roots stay anchored and sprout new hair from month 3.
What is the difference between FUE, FUT and DHI?
FUE extracts individual follicles. FUT uses a donor strip. DHI implants directly using specialized pens.
When can I resume work and exercise?
Desk work resumes in 3 to 4 days. Gentle washing begins on day 3. Avoid gym workouts for 2 weeks.
Can native hair continue to thin?
Yes. Non-transplanted native hair may thin over time, so supportive medical therapy is often advised.
Can beards and eyebrows be transplanted?
Yes. Micro-FUE reconstructs patchy beards, moustaches, and eyebrows using single-hair grafts.
How is graft requirement determined?
Doctors assess donor density, scalp surface area, and facial balance during trichoscopy.
