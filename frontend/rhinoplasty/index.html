The Procedure

What is rhinoplasty?

Rhinoplasty, often called a nose job, is an operation that reshapes the bone and cartilage of the nose. It can make the nose smaller or, less often, larger; straighten a crooked nose; remove a bump on the bridge; refine or lift the tip; or narrow wide nostrils. When surgery on the septum — the wall of cartilage and bone between the nostrils — is added to help you breathe, it is called a septorhinoplasty.

There are two main approaches. In a closed rhinoplasty all the cuts are made inside the nostrils. In an open rhinoplasty there is also a small cut across the strip of skin between the nostrils (the columella), which gives the surgeon a direct view of the tip. Your surgeon recommends the approach that suits the changes you want.

According to the NHS, the operation usually takes 1.5 to 3 hours and is normally done under general anaesthesia. A splint is taped over the nose for about a week, and it can take months for the final shape to show, because swelling settles slowly — especially at the tip.

Shape and function are closely linked: reducing a nose can narrow the airway, and a deviated septum can make a nose look crooked. That is why it helps that rhinoplasty at Kezza is led by an ENT surgeon, who looks at how your nose works as well as how it looks.

Common Goals

What rhinoplasty can change

Most people come with one or two specific concerns. Your surgeon will tell you which changes are possible for your nose and which are not.

A bump on the bridge

A dorsal hump can be smoothed by reducing bone and cartilage, giving a straighter profile.

The tip

A wide, drooping or upturned tip can be refined or rotated by reshaping the tip cartilages.

A crooked nose

A nose that is twisted from birth or after an injury can often be straightened, usually together with the septum.

Width and nostrils

Wide nasal bones or flared nostrils can be narrowed so the nose fits the face better.

Breathing

A deviated septum or other blockage can be corrected at the same time — a septorhinoplasty.

Overall balance

Small changes in length, projection or the angle with the upper lip can bring the nose into proportion with your other features.

Suitability

Is rhinoplasty right for you?

A good result starts with the right reasons and the right timing.

Rhinoplasty may suit you if

  • A specific feature of your nose bothers you — a bump, the tip, the width or a crooked line
  • You have a blocked nose and want your breathing assessed as well as the shape
  • Your nose has finished growing (usually by the late teens) and you are in good general health
  • You have realistic expectations and are having surgery for yourself, not to please someone else

Talk to your surgeon first if

  • You have injured your nose recently — your surgeon will advise when it is safe to operate
  • You smoke — smoking slows healing, and you will be asked to stop before and after surgery
  • You take blood thinners, aspirin or supplements such as fish oil (only stop them on medical advice)
  • You have a bleeding disorder, diabetes, high blood pressure or another long-term condition
  • You are pregnant, or worries about your appearance take up a lot of your day

Under-18s are assessed with a parent or guardian, and surgeons usually wait until the nose has stopped growing. If worries about your appearance are affecting daily life, it is worth talking them through before any cosmetic surgery.

Step by Step

Your rhinoplasty journey at Kezza

From the first conversation to your final review.

  1. 01Consultation

    Examination and goals

    The surgeon examines the outside and inside of your nose, asks about breathing, injuries and previous surgery, and talks through what you would like to change. Photographs may be taken for planning.

  2. 02Planning

    Your plan and quote

    You agree on the changes and the approach (open or closed), and receive your quote. Routine blood tests and a fitness check for anaesthesia are arranged.

  3. 03Preparation

    The weeks before

    Stop smoking, avoid medicines that increase bleeding only as your doctor advises, and arrange for someone to take you home and stay with you.

  4. 04Surgery day

    1.5 to 3 hours

    The operation is usually done under general anaesthesia. A splint is placed on your nose, sometimes with soft packing inside for a short time.

  5. 05First week

    Rest and protect

    Keep your head raised, use cold compresses around (not on) the nose, avoid blowing your nose and bending, and take the medicines prescribed.

  6. 06Follow-up

    Splint off and reviews

    The splint and any stitches are usually removed after about a week. Follow-up visits check healing while the swelling settles.

Recovery

Rhinoplasty recovery timeline

A general guide based on NHS advice. Your surgeon will give you instructions for your own operation.

  1. Day 1–3

    Swelling peaks

    Swelling and bruising around the eyes, a blocked feeling and some soreness are normal.

  2. Week 1

    Splint off

    The splint and stitches usually come off after about a week.

  3. Week 2

    Back to work

    Many people return to desk work within 2 weeks, once bruising has faded.

  4. Week 4–6

    Exercise

    Avoid strenuous exercise and contact sports for 4 to 6 weeks.

  5. Month 6–12

    Final shape

    Swelling can take up to 6 months to go completely; fine changes at the tip can continue for up to a year.

Surgical vs non-surgical (filler) rhinoplasty

Filler injections are sometimes called a 'liquid nose job'. They can disguise small irregularities, but they are not a substitute for surgery.

Safety

Risks and side effects of rhinoplasty

Every operation carries some risk. Knowing them helps you make an informed choice and spot problems early.

Expected after surgery

  • Swelling and bruising around the nose and eyes
  • A blocked nose for a few weeks while the inside heals
  • Mild bleeding in the first days
  • Numbness or stiffness of the nasal tip for some months
  • Tiredness for the first week or two

Less common risks

  • Heavy nosebleeds or infection
  • Breathing that is worse rather than better, or a changed sense of smell
  • Damage to the septum, including a small hole (perforation)
  • An uneven result, or a result you are not happy with — some people need a second operation
  • General surgical risks such as a blood clot or a reaction to the anaesthetic

Contact the clinic urgently if you have heavy bleeding that does not stop, a high temperature, increasing pain or swelling, or difficulty breathing after surgery.
