Types of acne

  • Comedonal acne: mainly blackheads and whiteheads, often on the forehead, nose and chin. Retinoids and salicylic acid are the usual first steps.
  • Inflammatory acne: red papules and pus-filled spots. Creams that reduce bacteria and inflammation are combined, and tablets are added if it is widespread.
  • Nodular and cystic acne: deep, painful lumps that are the most likely to scar. This type needs prompt treatment with a doctor, and the American Academy of Dermatology notes that isotretinoin is used for nodular acne that has not responded to other treatment.
  • Hormonal acne: often along the jawline and chin in adult women, sometimes with irregular periods. Hormonal treatments can help alongside skin treatment.

Using acne creams without irritation

Retinoids and benzoyl peroxide work well, but they can make the skin dry, red or flaky in the first few weeks, which is the most common reason people stop. A few simple habits help. Use a pea-sized amount for the whole face rather than dabbing it only on spots, because acne treatment is about preventing new spots as well as clearing existing ones. Start every other night if your skin is sensitive, apply a plain moisturiser, and wear sunscreen during the day because some treatments make the skin more sensitive to the sun. If irritation does not settle, tell your doctor rather than stopping; the product or the way you use it can be changed.

Acne and dark marks in Indian skin

In brown skin, a pimple often leaves a flat dark mark behind after it heals. These marks, called post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, are not scars, and they usually fade over several months once breakouts stop. Controlling acne early is the best way to prevent them. Sun protection helps marks fade faster, and your doctor may suggest ingredients such as azelaic acid or a course of medical peels. Squeezing or picking spots makes marks darker and longer-lasting, and can turn a mark into a scar.

Everyday habits that help

  • Wash your face twice a day and after sweating with a gentle, non-soap cleanser; scrubbing harder does not clear acne faster.
  • Keep hair oils and heavy styling products away from the forehead and face.
  • Choose non-comedogenic moisturiser, sunscreen and makeup.
  • Do not pick, squeeze or pop spots, especially deep ones.
  • Take medicines exactly as prescribed and finish the course your doctor advises.

What affects the cost of acne treatment?

The cost depends on the medicines you need, whether in-clinic treatments such as peels are added, and how many review visits your plan includes. Mild acne may only need a consultation, prescription creams and a follow-up, while severe acne usually needs longer treatment and closer monitoring. Your doctor explains the plan before you start.
