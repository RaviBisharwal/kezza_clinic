DHI vs FUE at a glance

DHI (implanter pen)FUE (pre-made sites)
Graft extractionIndividual follicular units with a micro-punchSame as DHI
ImplantationThe pen makes the site and places the graft togetherAll sites made first, grafts placed with forceps
Often suitsHairline refinement, dense packing, gaps between existing hairLarger areas, front and crown together, big sessions
ShavingLess shaving possible in selected small casesRecipient area usually shaved
SpeedSlower per graftFaster for large graft numbers
RecoveryScabs clear in 1 to 2 weeksSame as DHI

Does DHI give better results?

There is no strong evidence that one technique consistently grows more hair than the other. Graft survival depends mostly on how carefully the grafts are handled: how quickly they are extracted, how they are stored, how gently they are placed and how experienced the team is. A skilled FUE team with good storage can match the benefit that DHI's shorter out-of-body time is meant to give.

What changes the outcome far more is the plan: a hairline that suits your face and age, a graft count your donor area can safely supply, and treatment to protect the hair you still have. That is why the technique is chosen after your examination, not before.

Combining DHI and FUE

Many hair-loss patterns need both precision at the front and coverage behind it. In a combined plan, the doctor may use DHI to shape the hairline, where angle and density matter most, and FUE site-making for the mid-scalp or crown, where larger numbers of grafts are needed. Both use the same extraction, so the donor area heals in the same way.

Can DHI be done without shaving?

For small cases, such as filling a few gaps or refining the hairline, grafts can sometimes be placed between existing hairs so that only the donor area is trimmed. Larger sessions usually need the recipient area shaved so that every graft can be placed accurately. Ask your doctor whether a partial-shave plan is realistic for your case.

Choosing where to have DHI

DHI is a surgical procedure and should be planned and performed by qualified doctors in a sterile operation theatre. The ISHRS has warned about clinics where technicians carry out the surgical steps. Before you book, ask who will design your hairline, who will extract the grafts and who will implant them.
