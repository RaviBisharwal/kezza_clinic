Melasma: why it keeps coming back

Melasma is a long-term condition rather than a one-off problem. In a study of 1,001 people with melasma across ten Indian centres, 85% were women, heat from cooking fires or work was linked to more severe patches, only about one in five used sunscreen, and more than a quarter had used steroid or triple creams. Each of these points shapes treatment. Daily sunscreen, a hat and shade reduce the trigger; heat is reduced where possible; and steroid creams are stopped safely. Even with good treatment, melasma often returns in summer, in pregnancy or when sunscreen is skipped, so the plan includes what to do at the first sign of a flare.

Prescription creams: what they do and their limits

  • Hydroquinone is one of the most studied creams for melasma. It is used for a limited period, usually two to four months, and stopped if there is no benefit by three months. Long use of high strengths can cause a blue-black darkening called ochronosis, so it is not used open-endedly.
  • Triple combination cream (hydroquinone, a retinoid and a mild steroid) can clear or improve melasma in many people, but it is meant for short courses. The steroid can thin the skin and cause stretch marks and visible blood vessels if it is overused.
  • Azelaic acid, kojic acid, vitamin C and niacinamide are gentler options that are useful for maintenance and for sensitive skin.
  • Tranexamic acid tablets can help when other treatments have not worked. They are not suitable for people with clotting problems, a past blood clot, heart disease or a stroke, so they are prescribed only after a doctor checks your history, and melasma can return after stopping them.

Please do not buy steroid-containing creams for your face without a prescription. Fourteen topical steroids became prescription-only in India in 2018 because of misuse, and steroid damage to facial skin is much harder to treat than pigmentation.

Chemical peels and laser toning

Superficial chemical peels with glycolic, salicylic, lactic or mandelic acid loosen the outer layer of skin and help creams work. Indian guidelines consider superficial peels safe for Indian skin and do not recommend deep peels. Peels are usually spaced two to four weeks apart, after two to four weeks of skin preparation, and the strength is increased only as your skin tolerates it. Our guide to chemical peels for pigmentation explains the different acids.

Laser toning uses a low-energy Q-switched Nd:YAG laser over several sessions. Indian experts advise that laser should not be the first treatment for melasma, and studies report improvement ranging from about a fifth to three-quarters, with frequent relapse. Too many or too strong sessions can leave mottled light patches. For these reasons, laser toning is reserved for stubborn cases and always combined with sun protection and creams.

Pigmentation care for Indian skin

Medium and darker skin types make pigment easily, which is why they are more prone to melasma and to dark marks after acne or irritation. The same tendency means that aggressive treatment can backfire: a strong peel, a hot laser setting or a harsh scrub can cause new darkening. A safe plan starts gently, prepares the skin, increases strength slowly and treats irritation quickly. Your skin type, how easily you tan and how your skin has reacted to past treatments all guide the choice of creams, peels and settings.
