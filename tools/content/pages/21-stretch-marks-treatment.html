Red and white stretch marks need different plans

New stretch marks still have colour because of inflammation and blood vessels in the healing skin. At this stage, prescription retinoid creams such as tretinoin have improved early marks in studies, and collagen-building treatments tend to work better than they will later. Lasers that target redness are used for red marks in some countries, but they are not recommended for darker skin types, so for most Indian skin the focus is on microneedling radiofrequency, microneedling and gentle fractional laser. Old white marks have thin, flat collagen and few pigment cells. They need treatments that rebuild the skin's structure, and even then the change is gradual. Peels with glycolic acid combined with tretinoin, and microdermabrasion, can help the surface texture alongside these procedures.

In an Indian trial of 98 people treated with four sessions three weeks apart, fractional CO2 laser gave excellent improvement in more people than microneedling with PRP, but it also caused more redness and darkening. That trade-off is why the choice depends on your skin type and how much downtime you can manage.

Stretch marks after pregnancy

Stretch marks affect many women in pregnancy, most often on the tummy, breasts and thighs. During pregnancy and breastfeeding, cosmetic procedures and retinoid creams are usually postponed, and gentle moisturising is the safest option for itchy skin. Once breastfeeding has finished and your weight has settled for a few months, the marks can be assessed and treated. Marks that still look pink or brown at that point usually respond better than those that have already turned white.

Do creams and oils prevent stretch marks?

Many products claim to prevent stretch marks, but the evidence does not support them. A Cochrane review of six trials involving about 800 pregnant women found no high-quality evidence that any cream or oil prevents stretch marks, and studies of almond oil, cocoa butter, olive oil and vitamin E have not shown that they fade existing marks. Gradual weight change, gaining pregnancy weight within your doctor's advice and avoiding unprescribed steroid creams are more useful steps. Moisturisers can still make dry, itchy skin more comfortable.
