Treating pigmented dark circles

Pigment under the eyes builds up slowly, so it fades slowly too. Daily broad-spectrum sunscreen, including on cloudy days and indoors near windows, is the foundation, along with not rubbing the eyes. Your doctor may prescribe a cream to lighten pigment and repair the skin barrier. Mild chemical peels designed for the delicate eye area and laser toning can speed up the change over a course of sessions. In brown skin, stronger treatments are avoided because irritation can trigger more pigment.

Treating hollows and tear troughs

When the shadow comes from a groove rather than colour, adding a small amount of hyaluronic acid filler along the tear trough can smooth the transition from lower eyelid to cheek, so less shadow forms. Cleveland Clinic notes that not everyone is a good candidate, because some people become puffy after fillers in this area, and that the effect lasts about one to two years. The area has many small blood vessels, so tear-trough filler should only be given by an experienced doctor who can manage complications.

Home care that helps

  • Use sunscreen and sunglasses every day.
  • Avoid rubbing your eyes, and treat allergies or eczema that make you rub.
  • Aim for regular sleep, and try sleeping with your head slightly raised if you wake up puffy.
  • A cold compress for a few minutes can reduce morning puffiness.
  • Concealer with a peach or orange tone can hide brown-grey circles on Indian skin while treatment works.

Setting realistic expectations

Most people see a clear improvement rather than a complete change. Pigment tends to improve over months and needs maintenance, fillers change the shadow sooner but need top-ups, and inherited dark circles may always be slightly visible. Your doctor will explain what is likely for your type before you start.

What affects the cost of dark circles treatment?

The cost depends on the cause and the treatments needed. A home routine with prescription creams costs less than a course of peels or laser sessions, and filler is priced by the amount used. Your doctor explains the plan and the total after examining you.
