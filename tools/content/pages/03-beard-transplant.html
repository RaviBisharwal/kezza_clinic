Planning your new beard

A good beard transplant starts with design. The doctor looks at your face shape, your age, your existing facial hair and the style you like to wear, then marks the cheek line, jawline, moustache and any gaps to be filled. Because transplanted hair is permanent, the design should also suit you if you later choose to wear your beard shorter or change its style.

The number of grafts depends on the areas and the density you want. Filling a moustache, goatee or sideburns may need a few hundred grafts, while a full beard can need 2,000 or more. Your doctor will balance the look you want against what your donor area can supply.

Where do the grafts come from?

Most beard transplants use hair from the back and sides of the scalp, which is resistant to DHT and gives enough grafts for larger areas. For small corrections, hair from under the chin can sometimes be used, as its thickness and curl closely match the rest of the beard. The doctor chooses donor hair that matches the thickness and colour of your beard as closely as possible.

If you are also losing hair on your scalp, the donor area has to be shared carefully. A good plan keeps enough grafts in reserve for a possible scalp transplant in the future.

Areas a beard transplant can treat

  • Moustache: gaps or thin areas above the upper lip.
  • Goatee and chin: joining the moustache to the chin for a fuller goatee.
  • Cheeks: patchy areas and a clearly defined cheek line.
  • Sideburns and jawline: connecting the beard to the hair on the head.
  • Scars: many acne, injury and surgical scars where hair no longer grows.

Risks and how they are reduced

Beard transplants are generally safe when performed by trained doctors. Possible problems include small inflamed bumps or ingrown hairs (folliculitis), hairs growing at the wrong angle, temporary redness or pigment changes on darker skin, and tiny marks in the donor area. Careful graft placement, sterile technique and following your aftercare instructions reduce these risks. Tell your doctor about any skin conditions or medicines before surgery.

Grey or white hairs in the beard?

A transplant adds hair where it is missing; it does not change hair colour. If your concern is scattered white or grey hairs rather than gaps, white hair removal with electrolysis is the treatment to ask about, as laser cannot remove hair without pigment.

What affects the cost of a beard transplant?

The cost depends mainly on the number of grafts, which is set by the areas you want to fill and the density you want. The donor source, the technique and what is included, such as medicines and follow-up reviews, also play a part. Your doctor gives you a graft estimate after the consultation.
