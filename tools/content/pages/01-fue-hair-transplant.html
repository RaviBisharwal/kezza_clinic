FUE, FUT or DHI: how FUE compares

All three techniques move your own follicles from the donor area to the thinning area. They differ in how the grafts are taken and how they are placed, which affects scarring, speed and the kind of case each one suits best.

For most people who want to keep their hair short, FUE is the starting point. Your doctor may suggest DHI for a hairline or a combination of the two. Our FUE vs DHI guide explains the choice in more detail.

How many grafts will you need?

The number of grafts depends on the area to be covered, your stage of hair loss, the density of your donor area and the thickness of your hair. As a rough guide, restoring the hairline and temples in a man at Norwood stage 3 often needs about 1,500 to 2,500 grafts, and the number rises as more of the top and crown are involved. Your donor area sets the upper limit, so a good plan also keeps grafts in reserve in case your hair loss progresses.

These ranges are only a starting point. The doctor measures the area, checks your donor density with trichoscopy and discusses the density you want before giving you a graft estimate. Read more in our Norwood scale guide.

What affects the cost of FUE?

There is no single price for an FUE hair transplant, because no two cases need the same amount of work. The main factors are:

  • Number of grafts: the biggest factor, set by the area and the density you want.
  • Technique: DHI takes longer per graft than FUE, so it is often priced higher.
  • Donor area and hair type: thick, dense donor hair covers more per graft.
  • One or two sessions: large cases are often safer when split into two.
  • What is included: consultation, medicines, follow-up reviews and any PRP or GFC sessions.

Be cautious of a final price given on the phone without anyone examining your scalp. Our guide to hair transplant cost in Jaipur lists the questions to ask when you compare quotes.

Who should perform your FUE?

A hair transplant is surgery. The International Society of Hair Restoration Surgery (ISHRS) has warned about clinics where unqualified technicians perform the key surgical steps, with results such as poor growth, unnatural hairlines and over-harvested donor areas. FUE aftercare in brief

  • Sleep with your head raised for the first few nights and avoid touching or scratching the grafts.
  • Follow the washing schedule on your aftercare sheet; gentle washing usually starts around day 3.
  • Avoid the gym, swimming, heavy sweating and direct sun on the scalp for about 2 weeks.
  • Take the medicines as prescribed and keep your review appointments.

Protecting the hair you still have

A transplant does not stop pattern hair loss. Hair that was not transplanted can keep thinning, which is why doctors often combine surgery with medical therapy and treatments such as PRP or GFC. Your doctor will explain what suits your stage of hair loss.
