The short answer

A hydra facial, also called hydradermabrasion, is a machine-based facial that cleanses and exfoliates the skin, lifts debris out of blocked pores with gentle suction and then applies hydrating serums. A session takes about an hour including a skin check, usually with little or no downtime. It is a deep-cleaning and hydrating treatment for upkeep, not a cure for skin conditions such as acne scars or melasma.

How a hydra facial works

A handpiece with a spiral tip glides over the skin. As it moves, it releases a cleansing or exfoliating solution and, at the same time, draws loosened dead skin, oil and debris away with controlled suction. This avoids the pinching and pressure of manual extraction. Several companies make hydradermabrasion machines, and HydraFacial is the brand name of one of them; Kezza uses a different machine that works on the same principle. Research on hydradermabrasion is limited to small studies, so it is best thought of as a well-tolerated way to keep skin clear and comfortable.

What it can help with

  • Blocked pores and blackheads: suction extraction clears congestion without squeezing.
  • Dull or dry skin: exfoliation and serums leave the skin feeling smoother and looking fresher for a while.
  • Oily skin: regular sessions help manage shine and congestion between other treatments.
  • Before events: a popular refresher a few days before weddings and functions, because it usually causes little redness.

The strength of the peel and the suction can be lowered for sensitive skin, but any facial can irritate reactive skin, so tell the clinic about rosacea, eczema or recent reactions.

Who is a good candidate?

A hydra facial suits most adults with:

  • Congested pores, blackheads or oily skin.
  • Dull, dry or dehydrated-looking skin.
  • A wish for a refreshing facial without peeling or downtime.
  • Medical skin treatment in progress, when the doctor recommends gentle upkeep between sessions.

When should a hydra facial be delayed?

  • Active inflamed acne: red, painful pimples and cysts need medical treatment first; our acne treatment page explains the options.
  • Cold sores, rashes, sunburn or broken skin on the face.
  • A rosacea or eczema flare, or very reactive skin.
  • Recent peels, laser or strong retinoids, or isotretinoin, which make the skin more sensitive.
  • Allergy to aspirin (salicylates) or a past reaction to acid peels.

Clinic hydra facial or salon facial?

Salon facials often use steam, scrubs and manual extraction. Strong heat can aggravate melasma in pigmentation-prone skin, and rough squeezing can leave marks. In a clinic, the facial can be adjusted for acne-prone, sensitive or pigmented skin and combined safely with medical treatment when your doctor advises it. A hydra facial will not remove acne scars, melasma or deep lines; for those, see our acne scar treatment and pigmentation treatment pages.

Aftercare

  1. Use a gentle cleanser and moisturiser for the rest of the day.
  2. Apply a broad-spectrum sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher and avoid direct sun for a day or two.
  3. Skip scrubs, peels, retinoid creams, waxing and saunas for 24 to 48 hours.
  4. Leave makeup off for a few hours if you can.
