The short answer

Cryolipolysis (popularly called fat freezing) is an FDA-cleared, non-surgical procedure that reduces stubborn pockets of subcutaneous fat by exposing fat cells to controlled freezing temperatures. Fat cells are more vulnerable to cold than surrounding skin, nerves or muscle. The cold triggers programmed cell death (apoptosis), and the dead fat cells are naturally broken down and eliminated by your body's immune system over 8 to 12 weeks. It is designed for localized body contouring, not general weight loss.

The biological mechanism of cryolipolysis

Cryolipolysis was developed by Harvard photomedicine researchers Dr. Dieter Manstein and Dr. R. Rox Anderson after observing that children who ate popsicles sometimes developed small dimples in their cheeks (a condition known as popsicle panniculitis). They discovered that lipid-rich adipocytes crystallize at a significantly higher temperature than water-rich cells.

Common anatomical treatment zones

Cryolipolysis is effective for discrete, pinchable subcutaneous fat deposits that resist diet and aerobic exercise:

  • Submental region: Double chin fat that obscures jawline definition.
  • Abdomen: Lower and upper belly bulges below the navel.
  • Flanks (Love handles): Lateral fat resting over the pelvic rim.
  • Sub-scapular (Bra fat): Bulges along the mid-back and brassiere line.
  • Thighs: Inner thigh chafing zones and outer 'saddlebag' fat bulges.
  • Upper arms: Localized adipose tissue beneath the triceps.

Who is an ideal candidate?

Cryolipolysis produces the highest satisfaction in patients who meet specific clinical criteria:

  1. Within 10 to 15 kg of target body weight: The patient has a stable Body Mass Index (BMI < 30) and maintains consistent nutritional habits.
  2. Pinchable subcutaneous fat: The fat pocket must be soft and pliable enough to be drawn into the vacuum cup. It does not treat internal visceral fat (the hard, firm fat beneath the abdominal wall).
  3. Good skin elasticity: Skin should have sufficient tone to contract smoothly over the reduced adipose contour.

Who should not undergo cryolipolysis?

The procedure is medically contraindicated in individuals with cold-sensitive vascular disorders:

  • Cryoglobulinemia or cold agglutinin disease.
  • Paroxysmal cold hemoglobinuria.
  • Severe Raynaud's phenomenon in the treatment area.
  • Hernias in or adjacent to the treatment site.

Procedure steps and sensations

At Kezza Clinic, the treatment protocol follows four steps:

  1. Clinical marking: The doctor palpates the fat pocket, marks the boundaries of the adipose bulge and selects the corresponding ergonomic applicator cup.
  2. Gel pad barrier: A thermal-protectant anti-freeze gel pad is placed directly on the skin to insulate the epidermis from frostbite while permitting thermal conduction to deep fat.
  3. Vacuum cooling: The applicator draws the fat bulge into the cup. Intense cold is felt for the first few minutes, followed by complete numbness. Patients relax, read or use their phones.
  4. Post-treatment massage: Immediately after applicator removal, a two-minute manual massage breaks up crystallized adipocytes, significantly enhancing the apoptotic yield.

Maintaining your results

Because the destroyed fat cells are permanently removed by your body's immune system, the reduction in the treated pocket is lasting. However, cryolipolysis does not prevent remaining fat cells in untreated areas from expanding if calorie intake exceeds expenditure. Combining your contouring treatment with balanced protein intake, regular strength training, and proper hydration ensures lasting cosmetic definition.
