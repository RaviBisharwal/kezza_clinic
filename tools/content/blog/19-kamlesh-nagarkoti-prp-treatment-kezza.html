Why Athletes Choose PRP for Hair Loss

Professional cricketers face a unique set of challenges that can accelerate hair thinning: months of travel under harsh sun, helmet pressure on the scalp, high physical and mental stress, hormonal fluctuations from intense training, and disrupted sleep schedules. These factors combined can push hair follicles into a prolonged resting (telogen) phase, leading to noticeable thinning — particularly at the crown and temples.

PRP (Platelet-Rich Plasma) therapy is a preferred choice for athletes because:

  • Zero downtime — the patient can return to training immediately after the session.
  • No surgery — no incisions, no general anaesthesia, no recovery period.
  • Autologous (your own blood) — no foreign substances, no risk of allergic reaction.
  • Quick session — the full procedure takes 45 to 60 minutes at Kezza Clinic.
  • WADA-compliant — PRP is not on the World Anti-Doping Agency's prohibited list for most sports.

What Happens During a PRP Session at Kezza

Kamlesh's session at Kezza followed the clinic's standard PRP protocol, supervised by the medical team:

  1. Consultation — the doctor assessed Kamlesh's hair loss stage using dermoscopy (scalp magnification).
  2. Blood draw — a small sample of blood (approximately 15 to 20 ml) was drawn from the arm.
  3. Centrifuge processing — the blood was spun in a medical centrifuge to separate and concentrate the platelet-rich plasma.
  4. Scalp preparation — a topical anaesthetic cream was applied to the thinning areas for 20 minutes.
  5. Scalp injections — the PRP was injected in a grid pattern across the thinning zones using fine needles.
  6. Post-care — the doctor gave Kamlesh simple aftercare instructions (avoid washing hair for 24 hours, no heavy sweating for one day).

The total time from arrival to leaving the clinic was under an hour.
